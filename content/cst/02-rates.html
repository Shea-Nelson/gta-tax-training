Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

  • State the state, gross receipts and combined Florida CST rates.
  • Apply the separate rate for direct-to-home satellite service.
  • Decide whether the residential household exemption applies and what it removes.
  • Document a residential sale.

The Florida CST rate: 7.44%

Most communications services carry three state-level rates: {{cite:cst-page,gt800011,dr700016n}}

  • 4.92% state communications services tax (s. 202.12, F.S.)
  • 0.15% gross receipts tax (s. 203.01(1)(b)3., F.S.)
  • 2.37% gross receipts tax (s. 203.01(1)(b)2., F.S.)

Together they make the 7.44% Florida communications services tax. The 4.92% and 0.15% may be combined and treated as a 5.07% state tax, and the return is built around that pairing: one line for tax at 5.07% and another for tax at 2.37%. {{cite:cst-page,s202-12001,dr700016n}} The local tax is added on top (Module 3).

Direct-to-home satellite: 11.44%, no local tax

Direct-to-home satellite service is taxed at a state rate of 9.07% plus the 2.37% gross receipts tax, for 11.44%. {{cite:cst-page,s202-12,dr700016n}} No local CST applies to satellite service, because the law does not let local governments tax it. {{cite:s202-19,gt800011}} Satellite sales are reported on their own schedule, Schedule II of the return, with the 11.44% rate preprinted. {{cite:dr700016n}}

The residential household exemption

The separately stated price of communications services sold to residential households is exempt from the 4.92% state tax and the 0.15% gross receipts tax. The 2.37% gross receipts tax and the local tax still apply. {{cite:s202-125,dr700016n,cst-page}}

The exemption does not apply to: {{cite:s202-125,r12a19-041}}

  • mobile communications service
  • video service (cable or streaming television)
  • direct-to-home satellite service
  • any residence that is all or part of a transient public lodging establishment under Chapter 509, F.S.

In practice, then, the exemption covers home landline and similar fixed services such as home VoIP lines. A transient public lodging establishment is a unit or building that is advertised or held out to the public as a place regularly rented to guests, or rented more than three times in a calendar year for periods shorter than 30 days or one calendar month. {{cite:tip-10a19-03}} Since October 1, 2012, two kinds of establishments are no longer public lodging establishments and so could qualify for the exemption: apartment buildings designed for people aged 62 or older and inspected by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, and rooming houses, boardinghouses and other living or sleeping facilities not otherwise classified as transient public lodging establishments. {{cite:tip-12a19-03}}

Documenting a residential sale

Unless the customer says the exemption does not apply, the dealer need not collect the 5.07% when the service is sold at a rate based on a “residential schedule” in its tariff, or when it has on file a customer application or certificate representing that the service is for residential household use. An application can be information taken electronically or by phone in the ordinary course of business, and the dealer must accept it in good faith. A customer using the service in a transient public lodging establishment must tell the dealer, or the Department will look to the customer for the tax. {{cite:r12a19-041}}

Where residential sales go on the return

Because residential sales owe only the 2.37% and local tax, they are reported in Schedule I Column C (sales subject to 2.37% and local tax) but not in Column B (sales subject to 5.07%). {{cite:dr700016n}} Module 14 shows this on a full return.

Older rates

Before July 2015, the state portion was higher (6.65% from August 1, 2010, for a 9.17% combined state and gross receipts rate). {{cite:tip-10a19-05,tip-11a19-01}} Use the current return instructions, DR-700016N, for today's rates.

Key takeaways

  • 4.92% + 0.15% + 2.37% = 7.44% Florida CST; 4.92% + 0.15% may be billed as 5.07%.
  • Satellite: 9.07% + 2.37% = 11.44%, and never any local tax.
  • Residential landlines are exempt from the 5.07% only; 2.37% and local tax still apply.
  • Mobile, video, satellite and transient lodging get no residential exemption.
