Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

  • Describe the Address/Jurisdiction Database and how dealers and local governments use it.
  • Name the four methods that protect a dealer from liability for address errors.
  • Choose the right collection allowance, 0.75% or 0.25%, and know when it is lost.
  • Name the penalties for local tax reporting and assignment errors.

The Address/Jurisdiction Database

The Department's Address/Jurisdiction Database identifies the local taxing jurisdiction for Florida addresses. It is built from information the local governments provide and is updated every six months. {{cite:gt800011,pointmatch}} It is used three ways: {{cite:gt800011,dr700002}}

  • Anyone can look up an address to see its jurisdiction and CST rates.
  • Dealers can download the whole database, or one county or jurisdiction.
  • Local governments, after registering as users, can download their data and request changes.

Changes take effect only twice a year, on January 1 and July 1, so providers have time to respond. Local governments must post requested changes at least 120 days before the effective date, and approved changes are published about 90 days before it. {{cite:dr700002,dr700022}} A local government requests changes with Form DR-700022, Notification of Changes to the Address/Jurisdiction Database. Anyone who thinks an address is assigned to the wrong jurisdiction can file Form DR-700025, Objection to Address Assignment, and the local governments involved respond on DR-700026. {{cite:dr700022,dr700025,dr700026}}

Hold harmless: the four protected methods

A dealer that assigns a service address to the wrong local jurisdiction is held harmless from the resulting tax, interest and penalties if it uses due diligence in applying one or more of these methods: {{cite:s202-22,r12a19-070}}

  1. the Department's database;
  2. a database developed by the dealer or a vendor that the Department has certified (apply on Form DR-700012); {{cite:dr700012,r12a19-072}}
  3. enhanced ZIP codes (ZIP+4), with a reasonable methodology for ZIP codes that cross jurisdiction lines or rural addresses without postal delivery, based on the Department's or a certified database, local officials, or Census or Postal Service data; {{cite:r12a19-073}} or
  4. another database that does not meet (a) to (c) but meets the certification criteria when the Department audits.

The Department lists the vendors with certified databases on its CST page. {{cite:cst-page}}

The collection allowance

A dealer that files and pays on time keeps part of the tax as a collection allowance: {{cite:gt800011,s202-28,s202-22}}

Collection allowance rates
SituationAllowance
Dealer uses a qualifying method: the Department's database, a certified database, or ZIP+4 with a reasonable methodology (methods a to c)0.75% of tax due
Dealer uses any other method0.25%
Direct-to-home satellite provider0.75%

The allowance is not allowed if the return or tax is late. Since October 1, 2015, if only the payment is late, the disallowance is limited to the late percentage of the tax; a late return still loses the whole allowance. {{cite:s202-28,tip-15a19-02}} A dealer that uses qualifying and non-qualifying methods can file one return and take 0.25% on everything, or file two separate returns, one at each rate. {{cite:dr700020}} A dealer that fails to tell the Department it uses a qualifying method (Module 4) is assigned 0.25% at first, and moves to 0.75% once it provides the notice. {{cite:r12a19-020,dr700020}} No collection allowance is allowed on tax that a direct pay permit holder accrues on its own purchases (Module 12). {{cite:dr700016n}}

Penalties for local tax errors

  • $5,000 per return if a dealer fails to report and identify local CST on the proper return schedule, Schedule I. Leaving off Schedule I or using an unapproved spreadsheet in its place counts. {{cite:s202-28,dr700016n}}
  • 10% of the tax reported to the wrong jurisdiction, up to $10,000 per return, if a dealer that does not use one of the protected methods assigns addresses to the wrong jurisdiction. {{cite:s202-28}}

Lighthouse Mobile's allowance

Fictional business. Lighthouse Mobile uses a certified vendor database and has reported that on DR-700020. Its June return shows $40,000.00 of total tax (Line 5). {{cite:gt800011,s202-28,dr700016n}}

  1. Filed and paid in full by the deadline: allowance $40,000.00 × 0.0075 = $300.00.
  2. Filed on time, but $10,000.00 of the $40,000.00 was paid late: only 25% of the allowance is disallowed, so it keeps $225.00.
  3. Return filed late: no allowance at all.
  4. If it had used its own uncertified address list instead: $40,000.00 × 0.0025 = $100.00, and no hold harmless protection.

Key takeaways

  • The Department's database is updated every six months; changes take effect January 1 and July 1.
  • Four methods give hold harmless protection; three of them also earn the 0.75% allowance.
  • Other methods earn 0.25%; satellite providers get 0.75%.
  • Missing Schedule I: $5,000 per return. Wrong jurisdiction without a protected method: 10%, up to $10,000.
