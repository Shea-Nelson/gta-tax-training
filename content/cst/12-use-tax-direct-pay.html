Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

  • Decide when a purchaser owes CST use tax and which return to use.
  • Give the DR-700019 filing periods and due dates.
  • Explain the two kinds of direct pay permit and the annual limits for interstate permits.

Use tax: when the seller does not collect

If a dealer does not collect CST from a customer, the customer is responsible for paying communications services use tax. {{cite:cst-page,s202-151}} This most often happens when a business or household buys taxable service from a provider that is not registered in Florida. {{cite:tip-02a19-09,gt800011}}

  • Registered dealers report use tax they owe on their own DR-700016 return; they should not use the consumer form. {{cite:dr700019,cst-page}}
  • Everyone else files Form DR-700019, Communications Services Use Tax Return. {{cite:dr700019,r12a19-020}}

DR-700019 basics

The DR-700019 is filed semiannually. Returns for the period ending June 30 are due July 1 and late after July 20; returns for the period ending December 31 are due January 1 and late after January 20, moving to the next business day when the 20th is a weekend or holiday. {{cite:dr700019,r12a19-020}} The same rates and residential partial exemption apply as on a dealer's bill. {{cite:dr700019}}

Sawgrass Accounting's out-of-state VoIP

Fictional business and rate. Sawgrass Accounting, a firm in a city with a 5.22% local rate, buys a $300.00 a month VoIP plan from an out-of-state company that does not collect Florida CST. From January to June it owes use tax on $1,800.00: {{cite:dr700019,cst-page}}

  • 5.07%: $91.26; 2.37%: $42.66; local 5.22%: $93.96. Total $227.88.
  • It files DR-700019 for the period ending June 30 by July 20.

Compare: sales and use tax has its own use tax on goods and services bought without Florida tax, reported on the DR-15 or by consumers on DR-15MO. See SUT Module 5.

Direct pay permits (self-accrual)

A buyer of communications services can apply for a direct pay permit to accrue and pay the tax on its purchases directly to the Department, instead of paying it to the seller. Two situations qualify: {{cite:r12a19-030,gt800011,s202-12}}

  1. most of the communications services it buys are for communications that originate outside Florida and terminate in Florida; or
  2. the taxable status of the services will be known only when they are used.

Key rules: {{cite:r12a19-030,dr700030,dr700032}}

  • Apply on Form DR-700030. A permit is issued to a single legal entity, not to a group of affiliates.
  • The holder must be registered for CST (DR-700014); if not, it files DR-1 with the application.
  • A permit lasts five years. The Department sends a renewal notice, DR-700032, 60 days before it expires.

For holders of a permit for interstate services, the law caps the tax: the state and gross receipts tax on interstate services that originate outside Florida and terminate in Florida may not exceed $100,000 per calendar year, and a local tax on such services billed to a service address in a jurisdiction may not exceed $25,000 per calendar year. No refund is given for tax paid before the permit was received. {{cite:s202-12,s202-19}} Permit holders get no collection allowance on tax they accrue but do not collect from customers. {{cite:dr700016n}}

Key takeaways

  • When the seller does not collect, the buyer owes use tax.
  • Registered dealers use DR-700016; others use DR-700019, filed for June and December periods, late after the 20th.
  • Direct pay permits: mostly inbound interstate service, or taxability known only on use. Apply on DR-700030, renew on DR-700032.
  • Interstate permit caps: $100,000 a year state, $25,000 a year local.
