Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

  • List the buyers and uses that are fully exempt from CST.
  • Name the documentation a dealer needs for each exemption.
  • Explain when the Department looks to the customer instead of the dealer.

Fully exempt sales

Unlike the residential partial exemption (Module 2), these exemptions remove both the Florida and the local CST. Dealers should not collect tax on: {{cite:gt800011,s202-125,cst-page}}

  • sales for resale (Module 10);
  • sales or purchases of Internet access;
  • sales to the federal government and its agencies, the state, counties, municipalities and other political subdivisions;
  • sales to religious and educational institutions with 501(c)(3) status; and
  • sales to homes for the aged with 501(c)(3) status that meet certain conditions.

Direct pay permit holders also buy without paying tax to the seller, because they pay it directly to the Department (Module 12). {{cite:s202-12,r12a19-030}}

Government buyers

The exemption applies when the government entity pays directly. The dealer is relieved of collecting tax if it has on file either: {{cite:r12a19-042}}

  1. a written representation from the customer that it is a qualifying government entity, such as a customer application (which can be information taken electronically or by phone when the account was opened), a certificate, or a series of billing statements identifying the customer as such an entity and giving it a way to change that classification; or
  2. a copy of the customer's Florida Consumer's Certificate of Exemption (DR-14) identifying it as federal, state, county or municipal.

When a government pays with a Florida state or local government purchasing card (P-Card) marked for official business only, the dealer does not charge CST. It documents the sale with a copy of the card's face, or with the tax-exempt number, account number and cardholder name from it. {{cite:r12a19-042}}

Because the rule requires payment directly by the government entity, service that an employee pays for personally and is later reimbursed for does not meet that condition. {{cite:r12a19-042}}

Religious and educational institutions and homes for the aged

For a 501(c)(3) religious institution, the buyer gives the dealer a certificate signed by an authorized representative stating that the purchases are for a religious institution as defined in s. 202.125(4), F.S. The dealer does not need a copy of the IRS determination letter. {{cite:r12a19-043,tip-02a19-03}} Educational institutions follow a similar signed-certificate process. {{cite:r12a19-043}}

A home for the aged qualifies only if it is exempt under 501(c)(3), holds a valid DR-14, and either is licensed as a nursing home or assisted living facility or has at least 75% of its occupants aged 62 or older or totally and permanently disabled and qualifies for a property tax exemption. It too gives the dealer a signed certificate. {{cite:r12a19-043,tip-03a19-07}}

Internet access providers' purchases

Since July 1, 2008, under the federal Internet Tax Freedom Act Amendments of 2007, communications services that an Internet access provider buys, uses or sells to provide Internet access are not subject to Florida or local CST. TIP 10A19-08 includes a suggested exemption certificate that the provider gives the selling dealer at the time of sale. {{cite:tip-10a19-08,gt800011,s202-125}}

Good faith and who pays when documentation is right

A dealer must accept the customer's representation in good faith. If the dealer met the documentation requirements and the Department later finds tax due, the Department looks to the customer for the tax, penalty and interest. If the dealer's records show it did not meet them, the Department looks to the dealer. Electronic storage of the records is acceptable. {{cite:r12a19-042,r12a19-043}}

Key takeaways

  • Government, 501(c)(3) religious and educational institutions, qualifying homes for the aged, Internet access and resale are fully exempt.
  • Government sales must be paid directly by the entity; P-Cards count as direct payment.
  • Religious, educational and home-for-the-aged buyers give a signed certificate.
  • Proper documentation shifts any later tax to the customer.
