Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

  • Define communications services tax (CST) in one sentence.
  • Name its three parts: the state tax, the gross receipts tax and the local tax.
  • Explain who collects the tax, who owes it, and why it must appear on the bill as two separate lines.
  • Trace a tax dollar from the customer's bill to the state and local governments.

The one-sentence definition

Florida communications services tax applies to the sale of communications services that originate and terminate in Florida, or that originate or terminate in Florida and are billed to an address in the state. Communications services means the transmission of voice, data, audio, video or any other information or signal by any medium, which includes cable and streaming video, satellite, mobile, and Voice over Internet Protocol (VoIP) service. {{cite:cst-page,gt800011,s202-11,s202-12}}

So the test is about transmission. A telephone line, a mobile plan, a cable TV package and a satellite TV subscription all transmit signals, so all are communications services. Web hosting, Internet access and the phone itself are not, and later modules explain where those fall. {{cite:dr700016n}}

The three parts of the tax

CST is one tax with three components, imposed by two chapters of the Florida Statutes and administered together under Chapter 202. {{cite:cst-page,dr700016n,s202-12,s203-01}}

The state and gross receipts parts together are called the Florida communications services tax. The part a city or county levies is the local communications services tax. {{cite:cst-page,gt800011}} Module 2 explains the rates in detail and Module 3 covers the local tax.

Who collects it and who owes it

The business that sells the service, called a dealer or provider, must register, collect the tax from customers and send it to the Florida Department of Revenue. {{cite:gt800011,s202-17}} The tax itself is owed by the purchaser: a purchaser stays liable for the tax until it is paid, and paying it to the dealer relieves the purchaser of liability. {{cite:s202-16}}

A dealer may not advertise or state that it will absorb the tax or that the tax is not charged. The tax must be added to the price. {{cite:cst-page,s202-35}}

Two separate lines on the bill

Dealers must separately state the tax on the customer's bill as two items: the Florida communications services tax and the local communications services tax. {{cite:cst-page,s202-16}} A dealer may show the 4.92% state tax and the 0.15% gross receipts tax combined, as a single 5.07% “state tax” item. {{cite:s202-12001,s203-001}}

Exception: hotel and motel telephone charges

A public lodging establishment does not have to state the tax separately on a guest's telephone charges if it posts notice in each guest room that the charges include the tax. {{cite:s202-16,tip-02a19-01}}

How the money flows

The dealer files a monthly return, Form DR-700016, and pays the tax collected. If the return and payment are on time, the dealer keeps a small collection allowance. {{cite:dr700016n,cst-page}} The Department deposits the gross receipts portion as the State Constitution provides, distributes the remaining state portion under the law, and distributes each local government's tax through the Local Communications Services Tax Clearing Trust Fund. {{cite:s202-18,s202-193}}

Because local tax goes to the city or county where the customer's service address is, a dealer must assign every customer to the right jurisdiction. That is the job of the Address/Jurisdiction Database, covered in Module 8. {{cite:gt800011,s202-22}}

A short history

CST began on October 1, 2001, when a new law changed the structure and reporting of taxes on communications services. Its first rates applied to bills issued on or after that date. {{cite:tip-01ber-02,s202-20}} The rates have changed since then:

  • Until August 1, 2010 the combined state and gross receipts rate was 9.17%. On that date the state rate dropped from 6.8% to 6.65% and a new 0.15% gross receipts tax was added, keeping the 9.17% total. {{cite:tip-10a19-05}}
  • For periods from July 2015, the state portion is 4.92%, which is 5.07% when combined with the 0.15% gross receipts tax. {{cite:dr700016n}}
  • In 2012 the law renamed “cable service” as “video service” and added a definition of Internet access. {{cite:tip-12a19-04}}

Older TIPs and examples may therefore show rates such as 6.65% or 9.17%. Use them for how the rules work, not for today's rates.

Bayview Telecom's first bill

Fictional business. Bayview Telecom sells a $60.00 monthly business telephone line to a law office. On the bill it shows the service charge, then “Florida communications services tax” and, as a second line, “Local communications services tax” for the law office's city. It does not write “tax included” or “we pay the tax.” Each month it reports the sale on DR-700016. {{cite:cst-page,s202-16,dr700016n}}

Key takeaways

  • CST taxes the transmission of signals, including phone, VoIP, mobile, video and satellite service.
  • It has a state part, a gross receipts part and a local part.
  • Dealers collect it, state it on the bill in two lines, and cannot absorb it.
  • Local tax follows the customer's service address.
