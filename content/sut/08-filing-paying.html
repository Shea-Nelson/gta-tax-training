Learning objectives

  • Choose the right return (DR-15 or DR-15EZ) and filing frequency.
  • Meet the due date, including the earlier deadline for electronic payments.
  • Complete the DR-15 line by line and compute the collection allowance.
  • Recognize when electronic filing and estimated tax are required.

Which return

Sales and use tax is generally reported on the Sales and Use Tax Return, Form DR-15, with instructions in Form DR-15N. {{cite:sut-page}} Some businesses receive the simpler DR-15EZ. A business must use the DR-15, not the DR-15EZ, if it: {{cite:dr15ezn}}

  • paid $200,000 or more in sales and use tax during the state's prior fiscal year (July 1 to June 30);
  • sells or delivers taxable items into a county whose surtax rate differs from the rate where the business is located;
  • leases or rents transient accommodations;
  • sells aircraft, boats or boat trailers, or motor vehicles, mobile homes or other vehicles;
  • reports receipts from amusement or vending machines;
  • sells or buys untaxed dyed diesel fuel for off-road equipment or boats; or
  • claims any jobs, economic incentive or scholarship-funding tax credit.

Filing frequency

Most new businesses file quarterly. Frequency depends on the tax collected: {{cite:sut-page,gt300015}}

Filing frequency by annual sales tax collections
Annual sales tax collectionsReturn and payment
More than $1,000Monthly
$501 to $1,000Quarterly
$101 to $500Semiannual
$100 or lessAnnual

The Department reviews each account every year to set the next year's frequency and writes to businesses moving to monthly filing. To change frequency, call Taxpayer Services at 850-488-6800. {{cite:gt300015}}

Due dates

Returns and payments are due on the 1st and late after the 20th day of the month following each reporting period. A return is required for every period, even if no tax is due. {{cite:sut-page}}

  • Electronic payments (or filing and paying together electronically): start the payment and receive a confirmation number by 5 p.m. ET on the business day before the 20th. The Department publishes these dates each year in Form DR-659. {{cite:sut-page,dr15n}}
  • Electronic return without payment: if the 20th is a Saturday, Sunday or state or federal holiday, the return is timely if the confirmation number is received by the first business day after the 20th. {{cite:sut-page}}
  • Paper returns: if the 20th is a weekend or holiday, the return and payment are timely if postmarked or hand delivered on the first business day after the 20th. {{cite:sut-page}}

Electronic filing and payment

Any business may file and pay electronically through the Department's eFile and Pay webpage or vendor software. {{cite:sut-page}} It becomes required when a business paid $5,000 or more in sales and use tax during the state's prior fiscal year (July 1 to June 30): it must then file and pay electronically for the next calendar year, beginning with the January return filed in February. A business that is required to but does not is subject to a $10 penalty for failing to file electronically and another $10 penalty for failing to pay electronically, on top of any other penalty. {{cite:sut-page}}

The Department launched a new eFile and Pay system on December 1, 2025, and issued follow-up guidance for quarterly, semiannual and annual filers. {{cite:tip-25a01-16,tip-25a01-19}}

Paper filers who have no tax due and claim no deductions or credits can telefile at 800-550-6713 instead of mailing a return. {{cite:dr15n}}

The DR-15, line by line

The front of the DR-15 has five reporting lines, each with four columns: gross sales, exempt sales, taxable amount and tax due. {{cite:dr15n}}

DR-15 reporting lines (DR-15N, R. 10/25)
LineWhat goes there
ASales, services and electricity: sales and rentals of goods, admissions, amusement and vending receipts (other than food and beverage vending), taxable services, electricity, and dyed diesel.
BTaxable purchases: use tax (Module 5).
CCommercial rentals. The tax on commercial rentals was repealed effective October 1, 2025. {{cite:tip-25a01-04}}
DTransient rentals (Module 9).
EFood and beverage vending (Module 11).

Source: DR-15N. {{cite:dr15n}} Do not include tax collected in gross sales; doing so increases the tax due and leads to a bill. {{cite:sut-page}}

Then the summary lines: {{cite:dr15n}}

  1. Line 5, total tax due: the total of Column 4.
  2. Line 6, lawful deductions: for example, tax refunded to customers for returned goods, or tax paid on goods bought for use but sold instead. Deductions cannot exceed Line 5.
  3. Line 7, net tax due: Line 5 minus Line 6.
  4. Line 8, estimated tax paid and DOR credit memos: cannot exceed Line 7.
  5. Line 9, estimated tax due this month (estimated-tax filers only).
  6. Line 10, amount due.
  7. Line 11, collection allowance (electronic and on time only).
  8. Lines 12 and 13, penalty and interest if late (Module 17).
  9. Line 14, amount due with return.

The back of the return has the surtax lines 15(a) to 15(d) and Lines 16 to 21 for credits, electricity, dyed diesel and amusement machine receipts. {{cite:dr15n}}

The collection allowance

A dealer that files and pays electronically and on time may deduct a collection allowance of 2.5% of the first $1,200 of the amount due (Line 10), up to $30. There is no allowance for returns or payments made any other way. {{cite:dr15n}} The Sales and Use Tax webpage adds that the $30 limit applies for each reporting location. {{cite:sut-page}} A dealer may instead donate the allowance to the Educational Enhancement Trust Fund by checking the box on each original, timely electronic return. {{cite:dr15n}}

Computing the collection allowance

  • Amount due $800: 2.5% × $800 = $20.00 allowance.
  • Amount due $1,200: 2.5% × $1,200 = $30.00 allowance (the maximum).
  • Amount due $9,000: only the first $1,200 counts, so the allowance is $30.00.
  • Same $9,000, but paid by mailed check: no allowance.

Rule from DR-15N. {{cite:dr15n}}

Estimated tax

A dealer that paid $200,000 or more in state sales and use tax during the state's prior fiscal year (July 1 to June 30), not counting local surtax or transient rental taxes, must make estimated payments every month in the next calendar year, starting with the December return due January 1. {{cite:dr15n,gt300015}} The estimate is 60% of state sales tax due, computed with any of three methods, which may be changed during the year: {{cite:dr15n}}

  1. Average: 60% of the average monthly state tax due for the months with taxable transactions in the calendar year.
  2. Same month last year: 60% of the state tax due for the same month of the previous calendar year.
  3. Next month: 60% of the state tax that will be due on next month's return.

Failing to pay at least the minimum estimated tax brings a penalty for underpayment. {{cite:dr15n}}

Amended returns

An amended return replaces the original for the same period, so complete it as it should have been filed originally, not just the corrections. The quickest way is online; by mail, write "Amended replacement" on a blank return. Pay any additional amount with it; an overpayment becomes a credit. {{cite:dr15n}}

Bayside Bike Shop files for March

Fictional business, county with no surtax. Bayside files monthly and electronically. In March it had $40,000 in sales, of which $5,000 were sales for resale to other bike shops (documented with authorization numbers). It also used $500 of untaxed out-of-state parts in its own repair van.

  • Line A: gross $40,000; exempt $5,000; taxable $35,000; tax $2,100.00.
  • Line B: taxable purchases $500; use tax $30.00.
  • Line 5: $2,130.00. No deductions or estimated tax, so Lines 7 and 10 are also $2,130.00.
  • Line 11: 2.5% of the first $1,200 = $30.00 (filing and paying electronically by the deadline).
  • Line 14: $2,100.00, paid with a confirmation number before 5 p.m. ET on the business day before April 20.

Line rules from DR-15N. {{cite:dr15n}}

Key takeaways

  • Due the 1st, late after the 20th; e-payments must be started by 5 p.m. ET the business day before.
  • File every period, even with nothing due.
  • Electronic filing is required after a fiscal year with $5,000 or more paid.
  • Collection allowance: 2.5% of the first $1,200, up to $30, only if filed and paid electronically and on time.
  • Estimated tax applies after a fiscal year with $200,000 or more paid.
