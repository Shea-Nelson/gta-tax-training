Learning objectives

  • Apply a sales tax holiday correctly: eligible items, price limits and exclusions.
  • Know the 2026 holiday periods and where to find the TIP for each new period.
  • Recognize former holiday items that became permanently exempt on August 1, 2025.

How holidays work

A sales tax holiday is a period set by law when tax is not due on retail sales of listed items, often up to a price limit. Each holiday has its own TIP listing the items, limits and exclusions. Always read the current TIP, because the lists and dates change from year to year. {{cite:tip-26a01-11}} {{cite:tip-26a01-06}}

2026 Back-to-School holiday (July 20 – August 20, 2026)

No tax was due on: {{cite:tip-26a01-11}}

  • clothing, footwear, wallets, bags, handbags, backpacks, fanny packs and diaper bags priced at $100 or less per item;
  • certain school supplies priced at $50 or less per item;
  • learning aids and jigsaw puzzles priced at $30 or less;
  • personal computers and certain accessories priced at $1,500 or less, bought for noncommercial home or personal use.

The holiday did not apply to: {{cite:tip-26a01-11}}

  • briefcases, suitcases, garment bags, watches, jewelry and umbrellas;
  • skis, swim fins and skates;
  • items over the price limits;
  • cell phones, video game consoles and devices not primarily designed to process data;
  • computers bought for commercial purposes;
  • rentals and repairs of eligible items;
  • sales inside a theme park or entertainment complex, public lodging establishment or airport.

Dealers cannot opt out of the Back-to-School holiday. {{cite:tip-26a01-11}}

2026 Hunting, Fishing, and Camping holiday (September 1 – December 31, 2026)

This holiday covers listed camping, fishing and hunting supplies, each with its own price limit. For example, tents are eligible at $200 or less, and rods and reels at $75 or less individually or $150 or less as a set. The holiday does not apply to rentals or purchases for commercial use. See the TIP for the full list, including firearms, ammunition and accessories. {{cite:tip-26a01-06}}

Coastal Outfitters, December 2026 (fictional)

  • A $180 tent: no tax, because it is under the $200 tent limit.
  • A $90 rod sold alone: taxable, because it is over the $75 single-item limit.
  • A rod-and-reel set for $140: no tax, because it is under the $150 set limit.
  • A tent rented for a weekend: taxable, because rentals are excluded.

Former holiday items now always exempt

Since August 1, 2025, several items that used to be exempt only during disaster-preparedness holidays are always exempt. They include: {{cite:tip-25a01-05}}

  • AA, AAA, C, D, 6-volt and 9-volt batteries;
  • all bicycle helmets;
  • carbon monoxide alarms, fire extinguishers and smoke detection devices;
  • ground anchor systems and tie-down kits;
  • insect repellent and sunscreen;
  • Coast Guard–approved life jackets;
  • portable gas cans of 5 gallons or less;
  • portable generators of 10,000 running watts or less;
  • waterproof tarps of 1,000 square feet or less.

The gas-can exemption was expanded effective July 1, 2026. {{cite:tip-26a01-05}}

Source note

Key takeaways

  • Each holiday has its own TIP with items, price limits and exclusions. Read the current one.
  • Rentals, repairs, commercial purchases and items over the price limit are generally excluded.
  • Many former disaster-preparedness items have been permanently exempt since August 1, 2025.
