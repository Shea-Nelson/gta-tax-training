Learning objectives

  • Recognize the three common situations that create use tax.
  • Compute use tax with a credit for tax paid to another state.
  • Report use tax on Line B of the DR-15 and its surtax on Line 15(d).

What use tax is

Use tax is due on the use or consumption of taxable goods or services when sales tax was not paid at the time of purchase. {{cite:sut-page}} It is the mirror image of sales tax: when no seller collected Florida tax, the buyer owes it directly. Use tax is calculated at the same rate as sales tax. {{cite:arc-page}}

Three common situations

The Department gives these examples: {{cite:sut-page,gt300015}}

  1. You buy a taxable item in Florida and did not pay sales tax.
  2. You buy an item tax exempt intending to resell it, and then use it in your business or for personal use.
  3. You buy a taxable item outside Florida and bring it into, or have it delivered into, Florida, and did not pay sales tax (or paid less than Florida tax).

Why this matters in audits

Many businesses have a use tax liability, and the Department notes that one of the most frequent audit assessments involves unreported use tax. {{cite:gt300015}}

Rate

Registered dealers pay use tax at 6% plus surtax at the rate of the county where the item is used or consumed. {{cite:gt300015}} The New Dealer Guide describes use tax the same way: the 6% state tax plus applicable discretionary sales surtax. {{cite:gt800054}}

Credit for tax paid to another state

If the business paid sales tax to another state at a rate lower than Florida's, it reports the full Florida use tax and surtax and then claims the other state's tax as a lawful deduction. {{cite:gt300015}} The DR-15 instructions say to make sure the other state's tax was legally imposed and, when in doubt, to contact that state's tax agency. {{cite:dr15n}}

Harbor Print Shop (from the Department's own example)

Fictional business; the numbers follow the example in GT-300015. Harbor Print Shop is a registered dealer in a county with a 1% surtax (6% + 1% = 7%). It buys a $5,000 piece of equipment in New York and pays New York 4% sales tax ($200). {{cite:gt300015}}

  1. Florida tax and surtax: $5,000 × 7% = $350.
  2. Less tax paid to New York: $200.
  3. Additional tax due to Florida: $150.
  4. Surtax portion: $5,000 × 1% = $50.

On the DR-15 the dealer reports taxable purchases of $5,000 and tax due of $350 on Line B, a $200 lawful deduction on Line 6, and $50 on Line 15(d). Net effect: $150 owed for this purchase. {{cite:gt300015}}

Reporting use tax on the return

Line B of the DR-15 (Taxable Purchases) includes use tax on internet and out-of-state untaxed purchases. {{cite:dr15n}}

  • Columns 1 and 2 do not apply to Line B.
  • Column 3: the total of purchases used or consumed that were not taxed by suppliers and were not for resale.
  • Column 4: the use tax due, including surtax; also report the surtax on Line 15(d).

Source: DR-15 instructions. {{cite:dr15n}}

Consumers who are not registered dealers

Individuals who buy taxable goods through the internet, from out of state, from another country or by mail order without paying Florida tax can report the use tax for each quarter on the Out-of-State Purchase Return, Form DR-15MO, or pay online. The form multiplies the untaxed purchases by 6%, subtracts sales tax paid to another state, the District of Columbia or a U.S. territory (tax paid to a foreign country is not credited), and tells filers not to file when the result is less than $1. {{cite:dr15mo}}

Seaside Dental Office

Fictional business. Seaside Dental, a registered dealer in a county with no surtax, orders $800 of office chairs from an online seller that does not charge Florida tax. It also takes $50 of toothbrushes that it bought tax free for resale and hands them out free to patients.

  • Chairs: untaxed out-of-state purchase used in Florida, so use tax applies. $800 × 6% = $48.
  • Toothbrushes: bought for resale but used (given away) instead of sold, so use tax applies. $50 × 6% = $3. {{cite:arc-page}}
  • Line B, Column 3: $850. Column 4: $51.

Key takeaways

  • Use tax applies whenever Florida tax was not paid on a taxable item that is used in Florida.
  • Same rate as sales tax, plus surtax where the item is used.
  • Credit is allowed for a lower tax legally paid to another state.
  • Dealers report use tax on Line B; non-dealers can use DR-15MO.
