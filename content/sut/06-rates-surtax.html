Learning objectives

  • Find the right county surtax rate and know where the surtax is sourced.
  • Apply the $5,000 surtax limit, and know when it does not apply.
  • Compute tax with the rounding rule that replaced the bracket system in 2021.
  • Complete the surtax lines (15(a) to 15(d)) on the DR-15.

State rate plus county surtax

The total rate on a sale is the state rate plus the county surtax rate. {{cite:dr15n}}

State rate + surtax rate = total rate

The general state rate is 6% (3% for new mobile homes, 4% for amusement machine receipts, 6.95% for electricity). {{cite:sut-page}} Discretionary sales surtax rates depend on the county; they currently range from 0.5% to 2%, and some counties impose none. {{cite:dss-page}} New surtax rates take effect on January 1 and end on December 31 of the year the tax ends. {{cite:gt300015}}

Where to find the rate

  • Form DR-15DSS, Discretionary Sales Surtax Information, published each year and updated in November. {{cite:dss-page}}
  • The Address/Jurisdiction Database, which identifies the county for any Florida address and looks up the sales tax and surtax rate. {{cite:sut-page}} Its user guide is Form DR-700002. {{cite:dr700002}}
  • County TIPs. Each new, changed or expiring surtax is announced in a TIP. For example, on November 20, 2026 the Department issued TIPs on county surtax changes beginning January 1, 2027, such as an increase in Broward County. {{cite:tip-26a01-14}}

Check which DR-15DSS you are reading

When this guide's sources were retrieved (November 2026), the forms page linked the 2026 edition of DR-15DSS (revised 11/25) {{cite:dr15dss_26}}, while the generic "current" DR-15DSS file still held the 2025 edition (revised 11/24). {{cite:dr15dss}} The 2027 edition was still not posted when the forms page was checked again on November 22, 2026, even though TIPs issued November 20, 2026 announce changes for January 1, 2027. {{cite:tip-26a01-14}} Always confirm the calendar year printed at the top of the form, and check recent TIPs. This guide does not reproduce county rates for that reason.

When and where to collect surtax

Collect surtax when the transaction occurs in, or delivery is made into, a county that imposes a surtax and the sale is subject to state sales and use tax. {{cite:dss-page}} For goods, the rule is the delivery county:

A registered dealer that owes use tax on purchases also owes surtax if it is in a surtax county. {{cite:dss-page}}

The $5,000 surtax limit

Surtax applies only to the first $5,000 of the sales amount on the sale, use, lease, rental or license to use any item of tangible personal property. {{cite:dss-page}} Put another way, surtax does not apply to the amount above $5,000 on a single sale of a single item of tangible personal property. {{cite:gt300015}}

  • Several items can count as one item only if they are normally sold in bulk, or will be assembled into a working unit or part of one, and a written purchase order lists a definite quantity and delivery date. Deliveries in installments still qualify if made within the time the order specifies. {{cite:gt300015}}
  • The limit does not apply to admissions, taxable services, service warranties, prepaid calling arrangements, transient rentals, parking in lots or garages, docking or storage in docks and marinas, and aircraft tie-down or storage. Surtax is due on the full price of those. {{cite:gt300015,dss-page}}

Palm Furniture Gallery (the Department's dining set example)

Fictional business; numbers from GT-300015. Palm Furniture Gallery advertises, sells and invoices a $7,000 dining room set delivered to a county with a 1% surtax. {{cite:gt300015}}

  1. State tax: $7,000 × 6% = $420.
  2. Surtax: $5,000 × 1% = $50 (the set is sold as one unit, so the limit applies once).
  3. Total tax: $470.
  4. On the return, the $2,000 above the limit is reported on Line 15(a), and the $50 surtax on Line 15(d). {{cite:dr15n}}

Rounding: how to compute the tax on a sale

Since July 1, 2021, dealers must use a rounding algorithm instead of the old bracket system: carry the computation to the third decimal place, and if the third decimal is greater than 4, round up to the next cent. {{cite:sut-page,tip-21a01-02}} The rule can be applied to the total tax on all taxable items on an invoice, or to each item. {{cite:sut-page}}

The DR-15 instructions show the steps: multiply the taxable sale by the total rate, carry to the third decimal, and round. A $50.45 item in a county with no surtax: $50.45 × 0.06 = $3.027. The third decimal (7) is greater than 4, so the tax is $3.03. {{cite:dr15n}}

Older material may mention bracket charts

Before July 1, 2021, Florida used a bracket system to compute tax on fractions of a dollar. TIP 21A01-02 announced that rounding replaced it. {{cite:tip-21a01-02}} If you see a bracket chart in an older brochure or TIP, it describes the old method.

Cash transactions and the end of the penny

The tax computation above is separate from how a cash total is settled. After the end of penny production the Department issued guidance on cash transactions {{cite:tip-25a01-18}} and, in June 2026, on rounding cash totals to the nearest nickel. {{cite:tip-26a01-03}} Read those TIPs for how rounding a cash payment interacts with the tax on the sale.

Reporting surtax on the DR-15

Surtax collected or owed is included in Column 4 with the state tax, and the back of the return breaks it out: {{cite:dr15n,gt300015}}

  • 15(a): exempt amount of items over $5,000 (the part above the limit), included in Column 3.
  • 15(b): other taxable amounts not subject to surtax, included in Column 3.
  • 15(c): amounts subject to surtax at a rate different from your county's rate, included in Column 3.
  • 15(d): total discretionary sales surtax due, included in Column 4.

Key takeaways

  • Total rate = 6% + county surtax; surtax follows the delivery county for goods.
  • Surtax stops at $5,000 for one item of tangible personal property, but not for services, admissions or transient rentals.
  • Carry tax to three decimals and round up only when the third decimal is 5 or more.
  • Always check the year on DR-15DSS and recent county TIPs.
