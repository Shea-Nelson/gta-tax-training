Learning objectives

  • Explain what the Annual Resale Certificate (DR-13) allows and what it does not.
  • Document a sale for resale with any of the three accepted methods.
  • Tell the DR-13 apart from the Consumer's Certificate of Exemption (DR-14).

The Annual Resale Certificate in detail

The certificate allows business owners, or their representatives, to buy or rent property or services tax free when the property or service is resold or re-rented. Examples include items that become a component part of a product sold (nails, fabric and wood built into a chair), items resold or re-rented as tangible personal property, and services resold as part of regular business operations. {{cite:arc-page}}

It may not be used for property or services that will be used but not resold, used before being sold or rented, or used by the business or for personal purposes. If an item bought tax free for resale is used instead, the business must report and pay use tax on it. There are criminal and civil penalties for fraudulent use of a resale certificate. {{cite:arc-page}}

  • Signatures: not required. Presenting the certificate certifies that the items will be resold or rented. {{cite:arc-page}}
  • Getting the certificate: registered, active dealers get a new one each year. Electronic filers print their own; paper filers have it mailed with the coupon book or paper return. Certificates for the next year are available online each November. {{cite:arc-page,print-resale-page}}
  • New locations late in the year: certificates issued to new business locations beginning in mid-October are effective from the issue date through the following calendar year. {{cite:arc-page}}
  • Use tax only: a business registered only to pay use tax does not receive a resale certificate. {{cite:sut-page}}

Documenting a sale for resale: three methods

A seller must document each tax-exempt sale for resale with one of these methods, and may use a different method for each sale: {{cite:arc-page,gt300015}}

  1. Method 1: obtain a copy, paper or electronic, of the customer's current Annual Resale Certificate, and keep it for three years.
  2. Method 2: for each sale, get a transaction authorization number using the customer's certificate number, by phone at 877-FL-RESALE (877-357-3725), online through the Seller Certificate Verification application, or with the FL Tax mobile app. The number is good for that one purchase only.
  3. Method 3: each calendar year, get an annual vendor authorization number for each regular customer, by uploading a batch file online and retrieving the results 24 hours later.

With Method 2 or 3, the seller does not need to keep a copy of the certificate. The verification systems either issue a number or warn that the buyer does not have a valid certificate. {{cite:arc-page}}

Gulf Breeze Boutique buys inventory

Fictional businesses. Gulf Breeze Boutique orders 200 T-shirts to sell and one new cash register from Tampa Bay Wholesale.

  • T-shirts: for resale. The boutique gives its current DR-13 number; the wholesaler gets a transaction authorization number online (Method 2) and sells them tax free. {{cite:arc-page}}
  • Cash register: used by the business, not resold. The resale certificate cannot be used, so the wholesaler charges tax. {{cite:arc-page}}
  • Later the boutique gives three T-shirts to staff as uniforms. They were bought tax free for resale but used, so the boutique reports use tax on their cost. {{cite:arc-page}}

The Consumer's Certificate of Exemption

A Consumer's Certificate of Exemption (DR-14) is issued to eligible nonprofit organizations, religious institutions and governmental bodies, exempting them from tax on purchases for their own use. Payment must be made only by the entity named on the certificate; a personal payment that the entity later reimburses cannot be accepted. {{cite:gt800054}} Organizations apply on Form DR-5. {{cite:gt300015,dr5}} Sellers can verify both resale and exemption certificates online, by phone or with the mobile app. {{cite:dr15ezn}}

Key takeaways

  • DR-13 is for resale; DR-14 is for an exempt organization's own use.
  • Document every exempt sale: certificate copy, transaction authorization number, or annual vendor authorization number.
  • Resale certificates expire December 31 each year.
  • Items bought for resale but used instead owe use tax.
