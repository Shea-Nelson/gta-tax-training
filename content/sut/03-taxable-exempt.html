Learning objectives

  • Apply the "taxable unless exempt" rule to any sale.
  • Tell exempt groceries and medical items from taxable ones using Form DR-46NT.
  • Explain the three ways a sale becomes exempt: the item, the buyer, or the purpose (resale).
  • Use the decision flowchart to classify a sale.

The starting point: taxable unless exempt

A dealer is required to charge sales tax unless the sale is specifically exempt. {{cite:gt300015}} So the question is never "is there a law that taxes this item?" but "is there a law that exempts this sale?" When in doubt, the default is taxable.

For a complete list of exemptions, the Department points to the Florida Tax Handbook published by the Office of Economic and Demographic Research, which describes the exclusions, exemptions, deductions and credits from sales and use tax. {{cite:gt300015}}

Three ways a sale can be exempt

  1. The item is exempt. Examples are medicines and most groceries. {{cite:gt300015}}
  2. The buyer is exempt. Qualifying nonprofit organizations, state-chartered financial institutions and governmental entities can buy tax free when they hold a Consumer's Certificate of Exemption (Form DR-14). {{cite:gt300015}}
  3. The purpose is resale. A business buying items it will resell, or re-rent, buys tax free with its Annual Resale Certificate. {{cite:arc-page}}

Exempt sales still go on the return. They are reported in Column 2 (Exempt Sales) of the DR-15; examples the instructions give are sales for resale, sales of specifically exempt items, and sales to organizations that hold a DR-14. {{cite:dr15n}}

Decision flowchart

Groceries

Food items are generally exempt when sold in grocery stores to be taken home and prepared. Exempt examples include milk, butter, cereal, frozen goods and canned goods. Taxable examples include soap, paper goods, cleaning products and candy. {{cite:gt300015}}

The Department's Nontaxable Medical Items and General Grocery List (Form DR-46NT) gives a longer list and the key exception: food prepared and sold for immediate consumption, sold as part of a prepared meal (hot or cold), or sold for immediate consumption within a place that charges admission is taxable. Sandwiches sold ready for immediate consumption are taxable. {{cite:dr46nt}}

Medical items

Medicines are exempt. {{cite:gt300015}} DR-46NT also lists common household remedies that are exempt with or without a prescription, such as bandages, adhesive bandages, antacids, cough drops and pain relievers, and chemical compounds and test kits for human use, such as thermometers and blood sugar test kits. Test kits used for animals are taxable, and contact lens cleaning solutions are taxable. {{cite:dr46nt}}

Exempt buyers

To buy tax free, nonprofit organizations that meet the criteria in section 212.08(7), F.S., state-chartered financial institutions and governmental entities must first get a Consumer's Certificate of Exemption (DR-14) by applying on Form DR-5. {{cite:gt300015,dr5}}

  • The purchase must be paid with the organization's own funds. If a representative pays personally and is reimbursed later, the purchase is taxable. {{cite:gt800054}}
  • The U.S. government and its agencies are not required to hold a DR-14, although many do. If a federal agency has no certificate, keep documentation showing the sale was made directly to, and paid directly by, the agency. {{cite:gt300015}}

Exemptions change often

The Legislature adds and changes exemptions most years, and the Department announces each one in a Tax Information Publication. Recent examples include exemptions for baby and toddler products {{cite:tip-23a01-09}}, diapers and incontinence products {{cite:tip-23a01-10}}, oral hygiene products {{cite:tip-23a01-13}}, firearm safety devices {{cite:tip-23a01-14}}, a group of new exemptions beginning August 1, 2025 {{cite:tip-25a01-05}}, portable gas cans {{cite:tip-26a01-05}} and certain gold and silver coins beginning July 1, 2026 {{cite:tip-26a01-08}}. Before relying on any exemption, read its TIP for the exact definitions, dates and paperwork.

Key takeaways

  • Charge tax unless a specific exemption applies.
  • Exempt by item (groceries, medicine), by buyer (DR-14 holders) or by purpose (resale).
  • Prepared food and food sold for immediate consumption is taxable even in a grocery store.
  • Exempt sales are still reported, in the exempt column.
