Learning objectives

  • Decide whether an out-of-state seller must register under the remote sales threshold.
  • Explain who collects tax on marketplace sales: the provider or the seller.
  • Spot the delivery-network risk that local merchants face.

Remote sellers

Since July 1, 2021, a business not located in Florida that makes a substantial number of remote sales must register and collect Florida sales tax and surtax. {{cite:tip-21a01-03}}

  • Substantial number of remote sales: any number of taxable remote sales in the previous calendar year whose total exceeds $100,000. {{cite:tip-21a01-03}}
  • Remote sale: a retail sale of tangible personal property ordered by mail, phone, internet or other means from a seller that receives the order outside Florida and has the property shipped into Florida. {{cite:tip-21a01-03}}

The Department's sales tax page lists “selling into Florida as an out-of-state business having any number of transactions with total sales over $100,000 in the prior calendar year” among the activities that require registration. {{cite:sut-page}}

Marketplace providers and sellers

A marketplace provider lists or advertises a marketplace seller's goods and collects payment from the customer, directly or through others. A provider must register and collect if it has a physical presence in Florida or makes or facilitates a substantial number of remote sales. {{cite:tip-21a01-03}}

Not marketplace providers: {{cite:tip-21a01-03}}

  • businesses that only provide travel agency services;
  • delivery network companies not registered as dealers;
  • businesses that only process payments.
Who collects on a marketplace sale (TIP 21A01-03)
SituationWho collects and remits
A registered marketplace provider certifies to the seller that it will collectThe provider. The seller must not collect and leaves those sales off its own return.
A seller with a Florida physical presence or over $100,000 in remote sales outside the marketplaceThe seller must register and collect on those non-marketplace sales.

Remote sellers and marketplace providers must also collect surtax when delivering to a county that imposes one. They must register electronically and file and pay electronically. {{cite:tip-21a01-03}}

Delivery networks: a local-merchant trap

Unless a delivery network company elects to collect and remit Florida tax for its merchants, the merchant is responsible. Some networks do not collect at all. Others collect from customers and return the tax to the merchant, who must remit it. A network can elect to collect if it registers as a dealer and notifies all its merchants. {{cite:tip-23a01-24}}

Two businesses (fictional)

Peak Trail Gear is in Colorado. Last calendar year its Florida orders totaled $140,000, all shipped from Colorado and none through a marketplace. That is over $100,000 in remote sales, so it must register electronically and collect Florida tax plus the surtax of each delivery county. {{cite:tip-21a01-03}}

Tropic Tacos in Orlando sells through an app whose terms say it returns collected tax to the restaurant. Tropic Tacos must report and remit that tax. If it underpaid in the past, the Department points to voluntary disclosure as a way to fix it with most penalties waived. {{cite:tip-23a01-24}} {{cite:vd-page}}

Key takeaways

  • Remote seller threshold: more than $100,000 of taxable remote sales into Florida in the prior calendar year (effective July 1, 2021).
  • A marketplace provider that certifies it will collect takes over collection for marketplace sales.
  • Local merchants owe the tax on delivery-app sales unless the app has elected to collect.
