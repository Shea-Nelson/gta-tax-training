Module 16: Records and audits

Level: Master · About 25 minutes · Module 16 of 18

Learning objectives

  • Keep the right records for the right length of time.
  • Walk through the five stages of a sales and use tax audit and their deadlines.
  • Know the taxpayer's options: waiving the notice period, asking for a TAA, conferences and protests.
  • Explain the Certified Audit Program and audit sampling.

Records: what and how long

Records supporting all transactions in a reporting period must be kept for at least three years from the date the return was filed or was required to be filed, whichever is later. If a business fails to file, files a substantially incorrect return, or substantially underpays, the Department may audit periods longer than three years. [GT-300015] The DR-15 instructions state the same three-year rule. [DR-15N]

Records must be available to the Department on request. Failing to produce records, or filing a grossly incorrect or fraudulent report, can bring criminal or civil penalties. [GT-300015] Keep documentation for exempt sales, for example: [GT-300015]

  • copies of Annual Resale Certificates (DR-13) or authorization numbers;
  • copies of Consumer's Certificates of Exemption (DR-14);
  • signed statements or exemption certificates required for certain exemptions;
  • documents that show merchandise was shipped out of state.

Keep complete records of all sales and purchases, such as invoices, receipts, statements and register tapes. [GT-300015] If records are kept electronically, Florida law requires the business to make them available to the Department electronically. [GT-800022]

The audit, stage by stage

The Department describes an audit as an educational opportunity to find and fix bookkeeping problems that could cause additional liabilities. [GT-800022]

Same process for communications services tax: GT-800022 covers both sales and use tax and communications services tax audits. [GT-800022] The CST version, with CST-specific refund and penalty rules, is in CST Module 16.

Stages of a sales and use tax audit (GT-800022, R. 08/25)
StageWhat happensTiming
1. Pre-auditThe Notice of Intent to Audit Books and Records (Form DR-840) names the taxes and period. Gather the records on its attached list. The taxpayer may sign the DR-840 to waive the notice period and start sooner.60-day notice period
2. BeginningThe auditor holds an entrance interview to discuss the audit plan and the business. A Power of Attorney (Form DR-835) is needed for the auditor to talk with a representative.After the 60 days, or sooner if waived
3. DuringThe auditor reviews paper or electronic records, usually for a three-year lookback period. The taxpayer may request a Technical Assistance Advisement (TAA) on any issue.Assessment generally issued within 305 days of the DR-840
4. ConcludingA Notice of Intent to Make Audit Changes (Form DR-1215) summarizes findings. Tell the auditor whether you agree within 30 days. Signing it waives the audit conference but not the right to protest.Respond within 30 days
5. AfterA Notice of Proposed Assessment states the amount due and how to protest.Informal protest within 60 days; formal protest within 120 days

Source: GT-800022. [GT-800022] The Department also publishes an audit timeline (GT-800023) [GT-800023] and a general audit brochure (GT-800042). [GT-800042]

The Department's tips for a smooth audit

  • Keep records organized and provide information on time.
  • Ask questions.
  • Do not withhold information, change existing documents, or create documents that did not previously exist.

From GT-800022. [GT-800022]

Sampling

A detail audit examines every record and is the most accurate, but usually takes too long unless records are electronic. Auditors therefore typically examine a sample and project the results. The Department uses statistical and non-statistical sampling. [GT-800059]

The Certified Audit Program

Taxpayers can hire a qualified CPA firm to review their sales and use tax and local option tax compliance. In return, penalties are waived, the first $25,000 of interest is abated plus 25% of interest above that, and the Department will not audit the same tax and years except for fraud or misrepresentation. The incentives do not apply where tax was collected but not remitted. Only taxpayers that have not been mailed a DR-840 are eligible. [GT-800065]

Taxpayer rights

The Florida Taxpayer's Bill of Rights (GT-800039) summarizes taxpayers' rights in dealing with the Department. [GT-800039] Taxpayers can also request technical advice during an audit (GT-800061) [GT-800061] and use informal dispute resolution. [Informal dispute page]

Sunrise Café receives a DR-840

Fictional business. Sunrise Café receives a Notice of Intent to Audit on March 3.

  1. It has 60 days before the audit starts. Its owner wants to talk with the auditor now, so she signs and returns the DR-840 to waive the period. [GT-800022]
  2. Her bookkeeper will attend the entrance interview, so she files a DR-835 Power of Attorney. [GT-800022]
  3. The auditor finds untaxed purchases of kitchen equipment from an out-of-state supplier: unreported use tax, one of the most common audit findings. [GT-300015]
  4. She receives a DR-1215 and replies within 30 days. If she still disagrees after the Notice of Proposed Assessment, she has 60 days for an informal protest or 120 days for a formal one. [GT-800022]

Key takeaways

  • Keep records at least three years from when the return was filed or due, whichever is later.
  • Audits start with a DR-840 and a 60-day notice period.
  • DR-1215: respond within 30 days. Notice of Proposed Assessment: 60 days informal, 120 days formal protest.
  • Unreported use tax is one of the most frequent findings.

Self-check

Choose an answer for each question, then select Check answer. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

1. How long must records be kept?
Show the answer

Answer: At least three years from when the return was filed or due, whichever is later. Records must be kept at least three years from the date the return was filed or required to be filed, whichever is later.

2. What starts an audit?
Show the answer

Answer: A Notice of Intent to Audit Books and Records (DR-840). The Notice of Intent to Audit Books and Records (DR-840) starts an audit and its 60-day waiting period (GT-800022). The DR-1215 and the Notice of Proposed Assessment come later, at the end of the audit.

3. How long does a taxpayer have to file a formal protest after a Notice of Proposed Assessment?
Show the answer

Answer: 120 days. 60 days for an informal protest; 120 days for a formal protest (GT-800022).
