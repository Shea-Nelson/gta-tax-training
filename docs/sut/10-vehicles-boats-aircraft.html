Module 10: Motor vehicles, boats and aircraft

Level: Advanced · About 20 minutes · Module 10 of 18

Learning objectives

  • Source surtax on motor vehicles, mobile homes and boat-and-trailer sales to the buyer's residence.
  • Apply the partial exemption for vehicles sold to residents of other states (DR-123).
  • Know where use tax on boats and aircraft brought into Florida is paid.
  • Recognize the price rules for private (occasional) vehicle sales.

Surtax follows the buyer's home address

For motor vehicles and mobile homes, surtax is collected at the rate of the county in the owner's residence address on the registration or title, not the dealer's county. [SUT page] [GT-300015]

Since July 1, 2024, a boat and its trailer (where the trailer is a motor vehicle) sold to the same buyer, at the same time and on the same invoice, count as one item for the $5,000 surtax limit. Surtax is due at the rate of the county where the buyer lives, as shown on the registration or title. [TIP 24A01-11]

Department example (TIP 24A01-11)

A dealer in County A (1% surtax) sells a boat and trailer for $55,000. The buyer takes delivery at the dealer but lives in County B (1.5% surtax).

  • State tax: $55,000 × 6% = $3,300
  • Surtax: $5,000 × 1.5% = $75
  • Total: $3,375

The maximum state and surtax on the sale of a boat remains $18,000. [TIP 24A01-11]

Check this example with the Module 6 calculator: price 55000, surtax 1.5, one item of tangible personal property.

New mobile homes

Since July 1, 2022, the state rate on retail sales of new mobile homes is 3%, plus any surtax. Used mobile homes classified as tangible personal property stay at 6% plus surtax. [TIP 22A01-12]

Vehicles sold to residents of other states

Florida's 6% state tax is generally due when a vehicle is sold in Florida to a resident of another state. If the buyer's home state rate is below 6%, the buyer may claim a partial exemption. Florida then collects tax equal to what the home state would charge. [TIP 26A01-01]

  • The buyer completes Form DR-123 at the time of sale, declaring intent to license the vehicle in the home state within 45 days. It goes to the dealer, or for a private sale, to the tax collector or private tag agent. [TIP 26A01-01] [DR-123]
  • The tax collected is Florida sales tax, paid to the Department. [TIP 26A01-01]
  • Limits apply to corporations or partnerships with Florida-resident officers or 10% owners, unless the vehicle is removed within 45 days and stays out of Florida for at least 180 days. [TIP 26A01-01]
  • The Department publishes each state's motor vehicle rate in an annual TIP; this one is as of February 11, 2026. [TIP 26A01-01]

Private sales of vehicles

When a vehicle is sold by someone who isn't a dealer, both seller and buyer sign Form DR-99A to report the price when titling. If a reported price is below 80% of the average loan price for that model and year, the Department must request verification. Under-reporting brings additional tax, a 200% specific penalty, interest, and possible misdemeanor charges. [DR-99A]

Boats and aircraft brought into Florida

  • Use tax on a boat is paid to the county tax collector, a licensed private tag agency, or the Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles when the boat is registered in Florida. [Consumer page]
  • Use tax on an aircraft bought without Florida tax is reported on Form DR-15AIR, not the DR-15MO. [Consumer page] [DR-15AIR]
  • Items bought and used in another state, a U.S. territory or the District of Columbia for six months or longer before being brought into Florida are not subject to use tax. Items used in a foreign country don't qualify for this exception, except for vehicles of active-duty service members and their spouses used abroad six months or longer. [Consumer page]
  • Nonresidents who buy a boat or aircraft from a registered dealer for removal from Florida must give the dealer an affidavit and meet the removal and documentation deadlines. [TIP 24A01-10] [TIP 24A01-12]

Business vehicle leases

Long-term (12 months or more) business leases of motor vehicles are excluded from tax when the lessor paid tax on the vehicle. See Module 9. [TIP 24A01-08]

Key takeaways

  • Surtax on vehicles, mobile homes and boat-and-trailer combos follows the buyer's residence on the title or registration.
  • New mobile homes are taxed at 3%; used mobile homes at 6%.
  • DR-123 lets a nonresident pay their home state's lower rate (to Florida) if they license the vehicle at home within 45 days.
  • Boat use tax is paid at registration; aircraft use tax goes on the DR-15AIR.

Self-check

Choose an answer for each question, then select Check answer. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

1. A dealer in a 1% surtax county sells a car to a buyer whose registration address is in a 1.5% county. Which surtax rate applies?
Show the answer

Answer: 1.5%. For motor vehicles, surtax follows the owner's residence address on the registration or title.

2. A boat and trailer sold together on one invoice for $55,000 to a buyer in a 1.5% county. What is the surtax?
Show the answer

Answer: $75. Since July 1, 2024 they count as one item, so surtax applies to the first $5,000: $5,000 × 1.5% = $75 (TIP 24A01-11).

3. What is the state rate on a new mobile home?
Show the answer

Answer: 3%. Since July 1, 2022, retail sales of new mobile homes are taxed at 3%. Used mobile homes are taxed at 6%.

4. A resident of a state with a 4% rate buys a car in Florida and files a DR-123. How much Florida tax is collected?
Show the answer

Answer: 4%, paid to Florida. Under the partial exemption, Florida collects tax equal to the home state's rate, and the tax collected is Florida sales tax.
