Module 11: Food, beverages, restaurants and vending

Level: Advanced · About 25 minutes · Module 11 of 18

Learning objectives

  • Separate exempt groceries from taxable prepared food.
  • Apply the bakery rules: how food is packaged and where it is eaten.
  • Back tax out of tax-included receipts using the Department's divisors for concessions, vending and alcoholic beverages.

Groceries and prepared food

General grocery products are exempt. Food is taxable when it is: [DR-46NT]

  • prepared and sold for immediate consumption, except food prepared off the seller's premises and sold in the original container or sliced into smaller portions;
  • sold as part of a prepared meal, hot or cold; or
  • sold for immediate consumption in a place that charges admission.

Sandwiches sold ready for immediate consumption are taxable. Candy, meaning items regarded and advertised as candy on the label, is taxable. Tea is exempt unless sold in liquid form. Spreads are exempt unless they are cooked or prepared on the seller's premises. [DR-46NT]

Bakeries: a model for “how and where is it eaten?”

TIP 10A01-22 (issued December 21, 2010) explains bakery products. [TIP 10A01-22]

Bakery products: taxable or exempt?
Taxable when…Exempt when…
  • sold for consumption on the premises (five or fewer items are presumed to be eaten there);
  • packaged in a way consistent with eating on the premises; or
  • sold as hot prepared food, meaning kept warm or reheated by a heat source. Items still warm from the first baking are not hot prepared food.
  • sold to be eaten off the premises and packaged for that (glued, stapled, wrapped or sealed); or
  • sold by a bakery with no eating facilities.

Exempt take-out sales must be accounted for separately, for example with a separate register key or invoice notes. [TIP 10A01-22]

Morning Rise Bakery (fictional)

Morning Rise has café tables. A customer buys three bagels handed over on a tray: taxable, because five or fewer items are presumed eaten on site. Another customer buys a sealed box of a dozen donuts to take home, rung on the bakery's “take-out bakery” key: exempt. A toasted bagel and a coffee: taxable. These follow the patterns in TIP 10A01-22. [TIP 10A01-22]

When tax is included in the price: divisors

Concession stands, vending machines and bars often ring a single total that already includes tax. The Department publishes divisors to back out the tax. The divisors depend on the combined state and surtax rate where the sale happens. [GT-800003] [GT-800041] [GT-800046]

  1. Divide the total receipts by the divisor to get gross sales, which is also the taxable amount.
  2. Subtract gross sales from the receipts to get the tax due (state tax plus surtax).
  3. Multiply the taxable amount by the surtax rate to get the surtax for Line 15(d).

Department example: concession stand with exempt water

A concession stand in a 7% county takes in $100, including $25 of bottled water (exempt).

  • $100 − $25 = $75 taxable receipts
  • $75 ÷ 1.0751 = $69.76 taxable amount
  • $75 − $69.76 = $5.24 tax due
  • $69.76 + $25 = $94.76 gross sales
  • $69.76 × 1% = $0.70 surtax

Source: GT-800003. [GT-800003]

Alcoholic beverages

Alcoholic beverages, including mixed drinks, beer, ale, wine and packaged goods, are taxable plus surtax where sold. Complimentary drinks are subject to use tax on their total cost. When tax isn't itemized on receipts: [GT-800046]

  • Method 1, no notice that tax is included: multiply receipts by the effective rate. At 7%, that is 0.0730 for packaged goods only and 0.0751 for mixed drinks. Example: $2,000 × 0.0730 = $146.
  • Method 2, posted notice that tax is included: divide receipts by the divisor. Example: $2,000 ÷ 1.0730 = $1,863.93 gross sales, so $136.07 is tax.

Concessions, fundraisers and vending

  • Concession sales of food, drinks and other items priced at 10 cents or more are taxable. That includes fundraisers by nonprofit DR-14 holders unless a specific exemption applies. [GT-800003]
  • Bottled drinking water is exempt, but not if carbonation or flavor is added during manufacturing. [GT-800003]
  • A nonprofit DR-14 holder's concession sales are exempt only if every condition is met: no more than two fundraising events in 12 months, each lasting fewer than 31 consecutive days, not on premises shared with other businesses that collect sales tax, and tax was paid on the cost of items sold. The exemption does not apply to K-12 schools or PTOs and PTAs. [GT-800003]
  • K-12 schools, PTOs, PTAs and school support organizations may instead pay tax to suppliers on items they resell. They then do not collect tax at the event. [GT-800003]
  • Vending sales of 10 cents or more are taxable unless exempt. Food and drinks of 25 cents or less sold from machines sponsored by 501(c)(3) or (c)(4) organizations are exempt, as are food and drinks from machines in K-12 student dining areas (not public areas). The operator reports on gross receipts. [GT-800041]

Key takeaways

  • Groceries are exempt. Prepared food, candy and food sold for immediate consumption are taxable.
  • For bakeries, packaging, quantity and eating facilities decide the answer.
  • Tax-included receipts are backed out with the published divisors, never 1 plus the rate.

Self-check

Choose an answer for each question, then select Check answer. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

1. A bakery with tables sells three muffins on a tray. Taxable?
Show the answer

Answer: Yes, five or fewer items are presumed eaten on the premises. TIP 10A01-22: bakery products sold in quantities of five or fewer are presumed sold for consumption on the premises.

2. Concession receipts in a 7% county are $100, all taxable. What is the tax due?
Show the answer

Answer: $6.99. $100 ÷ 1.0751 = $93.01 gross sales; $100 − $93.01 = $6.99 (GT-800003).

3. A bar posts signs saying tax is included in drink prices. Which method does it use?
Show the answer

Answer: Divide receipts by the divisor. Method 2, used when customers are told tax is included, divides receipts by the divisor (GT-800046).

4. Is candy exempt as a grocery?
Show the answer

Answer: No, items advertised as candy are taxable. DR-46NT: candy and similar items regarded and advertised as candy are taxable.
