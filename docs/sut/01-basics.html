Module 1: What sales and use tax is and who owes it

Level: Beginner · About 20 minutes · Module 1 of 18

Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

  • Name the four kinds of transactions Florida sales tax applies to.
  • State the general rate and the three exceptions to it.
  • Explain the difference between sales tax, use tax and discretionary sales surtax.
  • Describe a dealer's role as an agent of the state.

The one-sentence definition

In Florida, each sale, admission, storage, or rental is taxable unless the transaction is exempt. The tax is added to the price of taxable goods or services and collected from the purchaser at the time of sale. [SUT page]

Two ideas in that sentence matter for everything that follows:

  • Taxable is the default. A seller must charge tax unless the sale is specifically exempt. [GT-300015]
  • The buyer bears the tax, the seller collects it. The seller is not paying its own tax; it is collecting the customer's tax for the state.

Three related taxes

Sales tax, use tax and discretionary sales surtax compared
TaxWhat it isWho reports it
Sales taxTax on taxable sales, admissions, storage and rentals in Florida, added to the price. [SUT page]The selling dealer collects it from the buyer and remits it.
Use taxTax on the use or consumption of taxable goods or services when sales tax was not paid at the time of purchase, for example an out-of-state purchase delivered into Florida untaxed. [SUT page]The buyer, because no seller collected Florida tax. Module 5 covers it.
Discretionary sales surtaxA county tax added to most transactions subject to sales or use tax, at a rate set by each county that imposes one. [SUT page]Collected and reported with the state tax on the same return; the Department distributes it to the counties. [Surtax page]

The rates

Florida's general state sales tax rate is 6%, with these exceptions: [SUT page]

  • Retail sales of new mobile homes: 3%
  • Amusement machine receipts: 4%
  • Electricity: 6.95%, which is 2.6% imposed under Chapter 203, F.S., plus 4.35% imposed under Chapter 212, F.S. [DR-15N]

Most counties add a discretionary sales surtax. Rates and the special $5,000 limit are covered in Module 6.

What kinds of business activity are taxable

The Department lists these business activities as requiring sales and use tax registration (the list is partial): [SUT page]

  • Selling taxable items at retail
  • Repairing or altering tangible personal property
  • Renting short-term living accommodations, such as hotel rooms, beach houses, condominiums, timeshare resorts, vacation houses or travel parks
  • Renting or leasing personal property such as vehicles, machinery or equipment
  • Charging admission to any place of amusement, sport or recreation
  • Manufacturing or producing goods for retail sale
  • Selling service warranty contracts
  • Operating vending or amusement machines
  • Providing taxable services, such as investigative and crime protection services, interior nonresidential cleaning, or nonresidential pest control
  • Selling into Florida from out of state with total remote sales over $100,000 in the prior calendar year
  • Selling through marketplace providers that facilitate remote sales into Florida

Key term: tangible personal property

Personal property that you can see, weigh, measure or touch, or that is in any manner perceptible to the senses, including electricity. [Surtax page] Most taxable sales involve tangible personal property. Most services are not taxable in Florida unless the law names them; see Module 4.

The dealer is an agent of the state

A registered dealer acts as an agent of the State of Florida and must collect state sales tax plus any county surtax on each taxable transaction, pay use tax on items bought tax exempt but used in the business, remit what it collects and owes, and keep records for at least three years. [GT-300015]

Sales tax and surtax a business collects become state funds at the moment of collection. The dealer is a custodian of those funds until it remits them, and the Department asks dealers to keep a separate accounting of them. [GT-300015] [GT-800054]

Showing tax on the receipt

Anyone making taxable sales in Florida must separately state the Florida sales tax on each customer's invoice, receipt or other evidence of sale. Sales tax and surtax may be shown as one total or separately. [SUT page] Some industries, such as alcoholic beverage sellers, concession stands and vending machine operators, may use effective tax rates and divisors when separately stating tax is impractical; Module 11 explains how. [SUT page]

Sunny Shell Gifts

Fictional business. Sunny Shell Gifts sells a beach towel for $20.00 to a customer who takes it home from the shop in a county with no surtax.

  1. Is it a sale of tangible personal property in Florida? Yes.
  2. Is a beach towel specifically exempt? Nothing in the sources lists towels as exempt, so the default applies: taxable.
  3. Tax: $20.00 × 6% = $1.20. The receipt shows $20.00, tax $1.20, total $21.20.
  4. The $1.20 is state money from the moment it is collected. Sunny Shell reports it on its next return.

Where the law lives

The tax is imposed by Chapter 212, Florida Statutes [Ch. 212, F.S.], and administered under the Department's rules in Chapter 12A-1, Florida Administrative Code [Rule 12A-1, F.A.C.]. The Department explains the law in forms, brochures and Tax Information Publications (TIPs), which announce changes and clarify how the law applies. [TIPs page] This guide cites those official explanations; the statutes and rules control.

Key takeaways

  • Every sale, admission, storage or rental in Florida is taxable unless exempt.
  • The general state rate is 6%, with 3% for new mobile homes, 4% for amusement machine receipts, and 6.95% for electricity.
  • Use tax covers untaxed purchases; surtax is the county add-on.
  • Collected tax is state money held in trust by the dealer.

Self-check

Choose an answer for each question, then select Check answer. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

1. A Florida shop sells a taxable item and does not know of any exemption for it. What should it do?
Show the answer

Answer: Charge tax, because sales are taxable unless specifically exempt. Each sale, admission, storage or rental is taxable unless the transaction is exempt, so a dealer charges tax unless a specific exemption applies.

2. What is the state sales tax rate on electricity?
Show the answer

Answer: 6.95%. Electricity is taxed at 6.95% (2.6% under Chapter 203 plus 4.35% under Chapter 212), plus surtax.

3. A business buys office chairs from an out-of-state website that charges no Florida tax. Which tax applies?
Show the answer

Answer: Use tax, reported by the buyer. Use tax is due on taxable items used in Florida when sales tax was not paid at purchase.

4. When does sales tax a dealer collects become state money?
Show the answer

Answer: At the moment it is collected. Collected tax becomes state funds at the time of collection; the dealer holds it as custodian.
