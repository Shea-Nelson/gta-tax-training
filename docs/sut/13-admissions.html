Module 13: Admissions and amusements

Level: Advanced · About 15 minutes · Module 13 of 18

Learning objectives

  • Tax admissions correctly, including surtax sourcing and resold tickets.
  • Recognize the admissions exemptions enacted in recent TIPs.
  • Compute and report tax on coin-operated amusement machines, and know the certificate rules.

Admissions

Businesses that sell admissions to any place of amusement, sport or recreation must register. [GT-300015] Sales of admissions are taxable. [TIP 17A01-13]

  • Surtax: collected at the rate of the county where the event is held. [GT-300015]
  • No $5,000 limit: surtax applies to the entire charge for an admission. [GT-300015]
  • Food sold inside: food sold for immediate consumption where entry requires an admission is taxable. [DR-46NT]
  • Tastings: charges to attend a public wine or spirits tasting are taxable, even though the beverages provided by distributors are exempt. [GT-800046]

Resold tickets

When an admission is resold for more than its original price, the reseller collects tax on the higher price and may take a credit for the tax previously paid. Admissions bought by an exempt entity for exempt purposes are not taxable when the entity gives the seller a copy of its DR-14. [TIP 17A01-13]

A dealer that resells an admission to an exempt entity that doesn't resell it may claim a credit on Line 6 of the DR-15, or a refund on Form DR-26S, for the tax it paid. It must keep proof of the entity's exempt status and of the tax paid. [TIP 17A01-13]

Ticket resale (fictional)

Sunshine Seats buys a concert ticket for $100 and pays $6 in state tax (no surtax county, to keep it simple). It resells the ticket to a fan for $150 and collects $9. Sunshine Seats may credit the $6 it already paid, so it remits $3. The rule comes from TIP 17A01-13. [TIP 17A01-13]

Coin-operated amusement machines

Charges to use coin-operated amusement machines, such as arcade games, pool tables, jukeboxes and mechanical rides, are taxed at 4% plus surtax. Machines owned and operated by churches or synagogues are exempt. The operator calculates and pays the tax. Operators also pay tax on buying or renting machines, on parts and repairs, and on prizes. [GT-800020]

  • Who is the operator? If the business owner owns the machines, the business owner is the operator. Otherwise a written agreement decides. With no agreement, the business owner is the operator. [GT-800020]
  • Certificates: each location must display an annual Amusement Machine Certificate (DR-18C), obtained with Form DR-18. It costs $30 per machine at each location and expires June 30. A penalty of $250 per machine may apply for missing certificates, too many machines, or operating without a current certificate. [GT-800020]

Department example

Amusement machines in a 0.5% surtax county take in $100. $100 ÷ 1.045 = $95.69 gross sales. $100 − $95.69 = $4.31 tax due. $95.69 × 0.005 = $0.48 surtax. Report gross and taxable sales and tax on Line A, taxable amusement sales on Line 19 of a paper return, and surtax on Line 15(d). [GT-800020]

Key takeaways

  • Admissions are taxable unless exempt. Surtax follows the event's county, with no $5,000 limit.
  • Resellers collect tax on the higher resale price and credit the tax they paid.
  • Amusement machines are taxed at 4% plus surtax and need a $30 annual certificate per machine.

Self-check

Choose an answer for each question, then select Check answer. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

1. A concert ticket bought for $100 (with $6 tax) is resold for $150. What tax does the reseller remit, assuming no surtax?
Show the answer

Answer: $3, after crediting the $6 already paid. TIP 17A01-13: collect tax on the higher price ($9) and credit the tax previously paid ($6).

2. What is the tax rate on coin-operated amusement machine receipts?
Show the answer

Answer: 4% plus surtax. GT-800020: amusement machine charges are taxed at 4% plus any surtax.

3. Are Florida State Parks admissions taxable after July 1, 2025?
Show the answer

Answer: No, they are exempt. TIP 25A01-07: State Parks admissions, including annual passes, are exempt from July 1, 2025.
