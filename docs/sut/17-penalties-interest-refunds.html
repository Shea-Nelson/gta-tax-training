Module 17: Penalties, interest, refunds and voluntary disclosure

Level: Master · About 30 minutes · Module 17 of 18

Learning objectives

  • Compute the late-filing penalty, including the minimum.
  • Compute interest with the daily interest rate factor.
  • Choose between a credit on the next return and a refund application (DR-26S), and know the time limit.
  • Explain voluntary disclosure and who qualifies.

Penalties

If a return is late, a late penalty of 10% of the tax owed is added, with a minimum of $50. The $50 minimum applies even if no tax is due. Penalty also applies to incomplete returns, and interest accrues on late payments. [SUT page] [DR-15N]

Businesses required to file and pay electronically face a separate $10 penalty for each failure: $10 for not filing electronically and $10 for not paying electronically. [SUT page]

Worked example: the $50 minimum

Fictional business. Coastal Kites files its quarterly return 12 days late with $180 of tax due. Ten percent of $180 is $18, which is below the minimum, so the penalty is $50. Had the return shown no tax due, the penalty would still be $50. [SUT page]

A late return also loses the collection allowance, because the allowance requires a timely electronic filing and payment. [DR-15N]

Interest

Interest is charged on taxes or fees not paid on time. That includes underpayments and collection allowances that were reduced or disallowed. Florida uses a floating rate updated each January 1 and July 1 and published in a TIP. [Tax and interest rates page]

To compute interest: [Tax and interest rates page]

  1. Get the daily interest rate factor for the period from the TIP.
  2. Count the days late. Start the day after the “late after” date and include the day the return and payment are postmarked or hand-delivered.
  3. Multiply: tax due × days late × daily factor. If the rate changes during the late period, split the days and use each factor.

The Department's own example

$1,000 of tax was due with a November 2013 return (late after December 20, 2013) and was paid March 10, 2014: 80 days late (12/21/13 – 3/10/14). $1,000 × 80 × .000191781 = $15.34. [Tax and interest rates page]

Worked example: Coastal Kites (fictional)

Coastal Kites' September 2026 monthly return shows $180 of tax and is late after October 20, 2026. It files and pays on November 1, 2026.

  • Days late: October 21 through November 1 = 12 days, counting the payment day.
  • Interest: $180 × 12 × 0.000301370 = $0.65 (from TIP 26ADM-02).
  • Penalty: 10% of $180 is $18, so the $50 minimum applies.
  • Total: $180 tax + $50 penalty + $0.65 interest = $230.65, with no collection allowance.

Sources: [TIP 26ADM-02] [Tax and interest rates page] [SUT page]

Fixing mistakes: credit or refund

A dealer that overpaid because of an error on its DR-15 may take a credit on its next return instead of requesting a refund. [Refunds page] To request a refund of an overpayment, a payment made when tax was not due, or a payment made in error, file the Application for Refund – Sales and Use Tax (Form DR-26S) online or by mail or fax. The same form covers discretionary sales surtax and solid waste fees. [Refunds page] [DR-26S]

  • Time limit: the Department must receive the refund claim within three years of the date the tax was paid. Some claims have different limits. [Refunds page]
  • Documentation: the application is not valid until all required supporting documents are received. The Department may ask for more within 30 days. Form DR-26SN lists what each type of claim needs. [Refunds page] [DR-26SN]
  • Bad debts: a dealer that paid tax on a sale that became a worthless account may claim a credit or refund within 12 months after the month the bad debt is charged off for federal income tax purposes. [DR-26SN]
  • Sampling: a refund may be established by a statistical sample approved through Form DR-370060. [Refunds page]

A business that collected too much tax from a customer must return it to the customer or send it to the Department. [GT-300015] To correct a filed DR-15, complete an amended return as it should have been filed originally, not just the changed figures. The quickest way to file it is online, where you can pay any additional tax or report an overpayment. [DR-15N]

Voluntary disclosure

Taxpayers can self-disclose and pay unpaid or underpaid tax through the voluntary disclosure program. [Voluntary disclosure page]

  • Who qualifies: a taxpayer the Department has not previously contacted about the liability. Delinquencies that would routinely produce a bill anyway are not eligible.
  • Benefit: once tax and interest are paid, all penalties are waived unless tax was collected and not remitted. In that case a 5% penalty applies unless there is reasonable cause.
  • Lookback: three years before the postmark date of the request.
  • How: by written request. A business that needs to register should send a paper DR-1 with the request and should not register online.

All details above are from the voluntary disclosure page. [Voluntary disclosure page]

Collection matters

Unpaid liabilities can lead to collection actions. The Department's collection page explains payment options and how to resolve a debt. [Collection process page]

Key takeaways

  • Late penalty: 10% of the tax, minimum $50, even when no tax is due.
  • Interest = tax × days late × daily factor; rates change each January 1 and July 1.
  • Refund claims (DR-26S) must be received within three years of payment; DR-15 errors can be credited on the next return.
  • Voluntary disclosure waives penalties for taxpayers not yet contacted, with a three-year lookback.

Self-check

Choose an answer for each question, then select Check answer. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

1. A return with $200 of tax due is filed late. What is the penalty?
Show the answer

Answer: $50. The penalty is 10% of the amount due, with a $50 minimum.

2. $2,000 of tax is paid 21 days late at a daily factor of 0.000301370. What is the interest?
Show the answer

Answer: $12.66. $2,000 × 21 × 0.000301370 = $12.66 (the example in TIP 26ADM-06).

3. How long does a dealer have to claim a sales tax refund?
Show the answer

Answer: Three years from the date the tax was paid. The claim must be received within three years of the date the tax was paid (refunds page).

4. Who can use voluntary disclosure?
Show the answer

Answer: Taxpayers the Department has not contacted about the liability. Eligibility requires that the Department has not previously contacted the taxpayer about the liability.
