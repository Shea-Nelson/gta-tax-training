Florida Sales and Use Tax training guide

This guide teaches Florida sales and use tax from the ground up. It starts with what the tax is and who owes it, and ends with special industries, audits, refunds, and a capstone exercise in which you complete a full month of returns for a fictional business.

Who this guide is for

  • New staff who answer taxpayer questions or review returns
  • Business owners and bookkeepers who collect and remit the tax
  • Anyone who needs a structured path from beginner to expert

How the guide is organized

The 18 modules, plus Module 8A, a step-by-step companion on eFile and Pay, are grouped into four levels. Each module has learning objectives, plain-language explanations, at least one worked example with a fictional business, a self-check, and a list of the official sources it relies on.

Levels and modules
LevelModulesYou will be able to
Beginner1 to 3Explain what is taxed, register a business, and decide whether a sale is taxable or exempt.
Intermediate4 to 8, and 8AHandle services and use tax, compute tax and county surtax correctly, document exempt sales, and file and pay a return on time, electronically or on paper.
Advanced9 to 15Apply the special rules for rentals, vehicles, food, construction and agriculture, admissions, remote sellers, and sales tax holidays.
Master16 to 18Prepare for an audit, calculate penalties and interest, request refunds, and complete a complex return from start to finish.

All modules

  • Module 1: What sales and use tax is and who owes it

    The taxable events, the 6% state rate and its exceptions, dealers as agents of the state, and how the tax flows.

    Beginner · about 20 minutes

  • Module 2: Registering a business

    Who must register, Form DR-1 and the online application, what a new dealer receives, and when to update or re-register.

    Beginner · about 20 minutes

  • Module 3: Taxable and exempt sales

    Tangible personal property, groceries and medicine, exempt buyers, and a decision flowchart for any sale.

    Beginner · about 30 minutes

  • Module 4: Taxable services

    Which services are taxable in Florida, why most are not, and how repairs, cleaning and service warranties are treated.

    Intermediate · about 20 minutes

  • Module 5: Use tax

    When use tax is owed, the credit for tax paid to another state, and how to report it on Line B.

    Intermediate · about 25 minutes

  • Module 6: Rates, discretionary sales surtax and rounding

    State rates, county surtax, the $5,000 limit, where surtax is sourced, the rounding rule that replaced brackets, and a calculator.

    Intermediate · about 35 minutes

  • Module 7: Resale and exemption certificates

    The Annual Resale Certificate (DR-13), the Consumer's Certificate of Exemption (DR-14), and the three ways to document a sale for resale.

    Intermediate · about 25 minutes

  • Module 8: Filing and paying

    Forms DR-15 and DR-15EZ line by line, filing frequency, due dates, electronic deadlines, the collection allowance and estimated tax.

    Intermediate · about 40 minutes

  • Module 8A: eFile and Pay, step by step

    The eFile and Pay system launched December 1, 2025: who must file electronically, logging in, filing a DR-15, paying, deadlines, canceling, consolidated and bulk filing, and getting help.

    Intermediate · about 35 minutes

  • Module 9: Transient rentals and real property rentals

    Short-term accommodations, local tourist development taxes, the repeal of the commercial rent tax, and parking and docking.

    Advanced · about 25 minutes

  • Module 10: Motor vehicles, boats and aircraft

    Where surtax is sourced, sales to nonresidents, and the special rules dealers must know.

    Advanced · about 20 minutes

  • Module 11: Food, beverages, restaurants and vending

    Grocery versus prepared food, bakery rules, and backing tax out of concession, vending and alcoholic beverage receipts with the published divisors.

    Advanced · about 25 minutes

  • Module 12: Construction, manufacturing and agriculture

    Materials that go into real property and public works, contractor-made asphalt, agricultural exemptions, and the Florida Farm TEAM Card.

    Advanced · about 20 minutes

  • Module 13: Admissions and amusements

    When admissions are taxable, where surtax applies, resold tickets, recent admission exemptions, and coin-operated amusement machines.

    Advanced · about 15 minutes

  • Module 14: Remote sellers, marketplace providers and delivery networks

    The $100,000 remote sales threshold, marketplace provider duties, and who remits on third-party delivery platforms.

    Advanced · about 20 minutes

  • Module 15: Sales tax holidays and exemption periods

    How holidays work, the annual back-to-school and hunting, fishing and camping holidays, and how to read a holiday TIP.

    Advanced · about 20 minutes

  • Module 16: Records and audits

    What records to keep and for how long, what to expect in an audit, and taxpayer rights.

    Master · about 25 minutes

  • Module 17: Penalties, interest, refunds and voluntary disclosure

    Late penalties, the floating interest rate, amended returns, refunds on Form DR-26S, and coming forward voluntarily.

    Master · about 30 minutes

  • Module 18: Capstone: a full month for a fictional business

    Put every module together: classify sales, compute tax and surtax, accrue use tax, and complete a DR-15.

    Master · about 45 minutes

Important notes on currency

Tax law changes often. Several rules in this guide changed in 2025 and 2026, including the repeal of the sales tax on commercial rentals effective October 1, 2025 [TIP 25A01-04] and new guidance on cash rounding [TIP 26A01-03]. The sources were retrieved in November 2026. Always check the Department's Tax Information Publications for changes after that date.
