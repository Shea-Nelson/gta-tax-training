Module 14: Remote sellers, marketplace providers and delivery networks

Level: Advanced · About 20 minutes · Module 14 of 18

Learning objectives

  • Decide whether an out-of-state seller must register under the remote sales threshold.
  • Explain who collects tax on marketplace sales: the provider or the seller.
  • Spot the delivery-network risk that local merchants face.

Remote sellers

Since July 1, 2021, a business not located in Florida that makes a substantial number of remote sales must register and collect Florida sales tax and surtax. [TIP 21A01-03]

  • Substantial number of remote sales: any number of taxable remote sales in the previous calendar year whose total exceeds $100,000. [TIP 21A01-03]
  • Remote sale: a retail sale of tangible personal property ordered by mail, phone, internet or other means from a seller that receives the order outside Florida and has the property shipped into Florida. [TIP 21A01-03]

The Department's sales tax page lists “selling into Florida as an out-of-state business having any number of transactions with total sales over $100,000 in the prior calendar year” among the activities that require registration. [SUT page]

Marketplace providers and sellers

A marketplace provider lists or advertises a marketplace seller's goods and collects payment from the customer, directly or through others. A provider must register and collect if it has a physical presence in Florida or makes or facilitates a substantial number of remote sales. [TIP 21A01-03]

Not marketplace providers: [TIP 21A01-03]

  • businesses that only provide travel agency services;
  • delivery network companies not registered as dealers;
  • businesses that only process payments.
Who collects on a marketplace sale (TIP 21A01-03)
SituationWho collects and remits
A registered marketplace provider certifies to the seller that it will collectThe provider. The seller must not collect and leaves those sales off its own return.
A seller with a Florida physical presence or over $100,000 in remote sales outside the marketplaceThe seller must register and collect on those non-marketplace sales.

Remote sellers and marketplace providers must also collect surtax when delivering to a county that imposes one. They must register electronically and file and pay electronically. [TIP 21A01-03]

Delivery networks: a local-merchant trap

Unless a delivery network company elects to collect and remit Florida tax for its merchants, the merchant is responsible. Some networks do not collect at all. Others collect from customers and return the tax to the merchant, who must remit it. A network can elect to collect if it registers as a dealer and notifies all its merchants. [TIP 23A01-24]

Two businesses (fictional)

Peak Trail Gear is in Colorado. Last calendar year its Florida orders totaled $140,000, all shipped from Colorado and none through a marketplace. That is over $100,000 in remote sales, so it must register electronically and collect Florida tax plus the surtax of each delivery county. [TIP 21A01-03]

Tropic Tacos in Orlando sells through an app whose terms say it returns collected tax to the restaurant. Tropic Tacos must report and remit that tax. If it underpaid in the past, the Department points to voluntary disclosure as a way to fix it with most penalties waived. [TIP 23A01-24] [Voluntary disclosure page]

Key takeaways

  • Remote seller threshold: more than $100,000 of taxable remote sales into Florida in the prior calendar year (effective July 1, 2021).
  • A marketplace provider that certifies it will collect takes over collection for marketplace sales.
  • Local merchants owe the tax on delivery-app sales unless the app has elected to collect.

Self-check

Choose an answer for each question, then select Check answer. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

1. An out-of-state seller had $90,000 of taxable remote sales into Florida last year. Must it register?
Show the answer

Answer: No, the threshold is more than $100,000. A substantial number of remote sales means more than $100,000 in the previous calendar year (TIP 21A01-03).

2. A marketplace provider certifies it will collect on a seller's sales. What does the seller do with those sales?
Show the answer

Answer: Leave them off its return and not collect. When the provider certifies, the seller may not collect and excludes those sales from its return.

3. A restaurant's delivery app returns the tax it collects to the restaurant. Who remits it?
Show the answer

Answer: The restaurant. Unless the delivery network elects to collect and remit, the merchant is responsible (TIP 23A01-24).
