Module 13: Filing and paying, including the new eFile system

Level: Advanced · About 30 minutes · Module 13 of 17

Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

  • Give the due date and late date of a CST return, and the electronic payment deadline.
  • Decide whether a dealer must file and pay electronically.
  • Walk through filing in the eFile and Pay system that CST moved to on June 1, 2026.
  • Explain the alternative-period basis and when it is late.

Due dates

CST is reported on Form DR-700016, Florida Communications Services Tax Return. Returns and payments are due on the 1st and late after the 20th of the month after each reporting period. If the 20th is a Saturday, Sunday or state or federal holiday, a return is on time if filed electronically, postmarked or hand-delivered on the next business day. A return must be filed for every reporting period, even if no tax is due. [CST page] [s. 202.27, F.S.] [DR-700016N]

Electronic filing and payment

Any dealer may file and pay electronically. A dealer that paid $5,000 or more in CST during the most recent state fiscal year (July 1 to June 30) must file and pay electronically during the next calendar year. [CST page] [GT-800011] [s. 202.30, F.S.]

When paying electronically, a dealer must initiate the payment and receive a confirmation number by 5:00 p.m. ET on the business day before the 20th to avoid penalty and interest. The Department publishes these dates each year on Form DR-659, Florida eServices Calendar of Electronic Payment Deadlines. [CST page] [GT-800011] [DR-659]

A December 2026 deadline

Illustration of the rules above. The November 2026 return is due December 1, 2026. The 20th, December 20, 2026, is a Sunday, so the return is on time if filed by Monday, December 21. An electronic payment must be initiated by 5:00 p.m. ET on Friday, December 18, the business day before the 20th. Check the DR-659 calendar for the official date. [CST page] [DR-659]

The eFile and Pay system for CST (since June 1, 2026)

On June 1, 2026, CST moved to a new eFile and Pay system. Dealers still log in with their existing user ID and password, or with their Business Partner Number and Contract Object Number. History in the old system was available only through September 30, 2026; copies of earlier returns can now be requested on Form DR-841, Request for Copy of Tax Return. [CST eFile and Pay page] [DR-841] Taxpayers can enroll for a user ID and password through eServices enrollment. [CST eFile and Pay page] [Enrollment page]

The Department's step-by-step guide (September 2026) gives this path: [CST eFile guide]

  1. From the File and Pay page, choose Communications Services Tax and log in.
  2. Choose Online Transactions, then File a Tax Return/Report, and select the filing period. “Original Return” is the only report type for CST.
  3. Review the Return Header tab. The DR700016 tab fills in automatically from the schedules.
  4. Schedule I: for each county, add a row choosing “State Portion – Col B” and enter Column B sales; then add a row for each local jurisdiction with its Column C sales and Column E local tax.
  5. Schedule I Summary: enter the state tax due (Line F3) and gross receipts tax due (Line G6), then Calculate.
  6. Complete Schedules II to V if they apply: satellite, satellite adjustments, state and local adjustments, multistate credits. Adjustment schedules can be used to amend a previous return.
  7. Review the summary, sign, submit, save the confirmation, and choose to make a payment.

Large filers can use Direct File (formerly SecureNet), which accepts a file in the Department's format, described in its file specifications. [CST eFile and Pay page] [Direct File specifications] For help with the new system, call Taxpayer Services at 850-488-6800 or email fdortaxpayerservices@floridarevenue.com. [CST eFile and Pay page]

In the sales and use tax guide: SUT Module 8A walks through the same eFile and Pay system for the DR-15 screen by screen, including payment-only, canceling a payment and troubleshooting.

The alternative-period basis

Since October 1, 2015, a dealer may elect an alternative-period basis: any month-long period other than a calendar month, ending on or after the 15th of the month, such as the 22nd of one month to the 21st of the next. The dealer must keep the period for at least 12 months and elects it on Form DR-700033. Tax for the alternative period is still due by the 20th of the following month. [TIP 15A19-02] [DR-700033]

Department example: Dealer ABC, Inc.

Dealer ABC elected a period of the 22nd through the 21st. Tax for October 22 to November 21, 2015 went on its November 2015 return, due December 20, 2015. That was a Sunday, so the deadline was Monday, December 21, 2015, and an electronic payment had to be initiated by 5:00 p.m. ET on Friday, December 18, 2015. [TIP 15A19-02]

Key takeaways

  • Due the 1st, late after the 20th; file every period, even with no tax.
  • $5,000 or more paid in the prior state fiscal year: file and pay electronically next calendar year.
  • Electronic payments: confirmation number by 5 p.m. ET the business day before the 20th.
  • CST uses a new eFile system since June 1, 2026; ask for old returns on DR-841.

Self-check

Choose an answer for each question, then select Check answer. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

1. When is a CST return late?
Show the answer

Answer: After the 20th of the month after the period. Due on the 1st and late after the 20th, with weekend and holiday rollover.

2. Who must file and pay electronically?
Show the answer

Answer: Dealers that paid $5,000 or more in the prior state fiscal year. The threshold is $5,000 paid during the prior July-to-June fiscal year.

3. By when must an electronic payment be initiated?
Show the answer

Answer: 5:00 p.m. ET on the business day before the 20th. A confirmation number must be received by 5:00 p.m. ET on the business day before the 20th.

4. A dealer had no taxable sales this month. Must it file?
Show the answer

Answer: Yes, a return is required for each period. A return must be filed for each reporting period, even if no tax is due.
