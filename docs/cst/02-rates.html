Module 2: The Florida CST rates and the residential exemption

Level: Beginner · About 25 minutes · Module 2 of 17

Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

  • State the state, gross receipts and combined Florida CST rates.
  • Apply the separate rate for direct-to-home satellite service.
  • Decide whether the residential household exemption applies and what it removes.
  • Document a residential sale.

The Florida CST rate: 7.44%

Most communications services carry three state-level rates: [CST page] [GT-800011] [DR-700016N]

  • 4.92% state communications services tax (s. 202.12, F.S.)
  • 0.15% gross receipts tax (s. 203.01(1)(b)3., F.S.)
  • 2.37% gross receipts tax (s. 203.01(1)(b)2., F.S.)

Together they make the 7.44% Florida communications services tax. The 4.92% and 0.15% may be combined and treated as a 5.07% state tax, and the return is built around that pairing: one line for tax at 5.07% and another for tax at 2.37%. [CST page] [s. 202.12001, F.S.] [DR-700016N] The local tax is added on top (Module 3).

Direct-to-home satellite: 11.44%, no local tax

Direct-to-home satellite service is taxed at a state rate of 9.07% plus the 2.37% gross receipts tax, for 11.44%. [CST page] [s. 202.12, F.S.] [DR-700016N] No local CST applies to satellite service, because the law does not let local governments tax it. [s. 202.19, F.S.] [GT-800011] Satellite sales are reported on their own schedule, Schedule II of the return, with the 11.44% rate preprinted. [DR-700016N]

The residential household exemption

The separately stated price of communications services sold to residential households is exempt from the 4.92% state tax and the 0.15% gross receipts tax. The 2.37% gross receipts tax and the local tax still apply. [s. 202.125, F.S.] [DR-700016N] [CST page]

The exemption does not apply to: [s. 202.125, F.S.] [Rule 12A-19.041, F.A.C.]

  • mobile communications service
  • video service (cable or streaming television)
  • direct-to-home satellite service
  • any residence that is all or part of a transient public lodging establishment under Chapter 509, F.S.

Why the return instructions say “cable service”

The residential exemption note in DR-700016N (R. 01/26) still lists “cable service” among the exceptions, while the same instructions use “video service” elsewhere. The law renamed cable service as video service in 2012, so read “cable service” in that note as video service. [DR-700016N] [TIP 12A19-04] [s. 202.125, F.S.]

In practice, then, the exemption covers home landline and similar fixed services such as home VoIP lines. A transient public lodging establishment is a unit or building that is advertised or held out to the public as a place regularly rented to guests, or rented more than three times in a calendar year for periods shorter than 30 days or one calendar month. [TIP 10A19-03] Since October 1, 2012, two kinds of establishments are no longer public lodging establishments and so could qualify for the exemption: apartment buildings designed for people aged 62 or older and inspected by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, and rooming houses, boardinghouses and other living or sleeping facilities not otherwise classified as transient public lodging establishments. [TIP 12A19-03]

Documenting a residential sale

Unless the customer says the exemption does not apply, the dealer need not collect the 5.07% when the service is sold at a rate based on a “residential schedule” in its tariff, or when it has on file a customer application or certificate representing that the service is for residential household use. An application can be information taken electronically or by phone in the ordinary course of business, and the dealer must accept it in good faith. A customer using the service in a transient public lodging establishment must tell the dealer, or the Department will look to the customer for the tax. [Rule 12A-19.041, F.A.C.]

Where residential sales go on the return

Because residential sales owe only the 2.37% and local tax, they are reported in Schedule I Column C (sales subject to 2.37% and local tax) but not in Column B (sales subject to 5.07%). [DR-700016N] Module 14 shows this on a full return.

Older rates

Before July 2015, the state portion was higher (6.65% from August 1, 2010, for a 9.17% combined state and gross receipts rate). [TIP 10A19-05] [TIP 11A19-01] Use the current return instructions, DR-700016N, for today's rates.

Key takeaways

  • 4.92% + 0.15% + 2.37% = 7.44% Florida CST; 4.92% + 0.15% may be billed as 5.07%.
  • Satellite: 9.07% + 2.37% = 11.44%, and never any local tax.
  • Residential landlines are exempt from the 5.07% only; 2.37% and local tax still apply.
  • Mobile, video, satellite and transient lodging get no residential exemption.

Self-check

Choose an answer for each question, then select Check answer. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

1. What is the total Florida CST rate on most services, before local tax?
Show the answer

Answer: 7.44%. Add the state parts: 4.92% state tax + 0.15% gross receipts + 2.37% gross receipts = 7.44%. 5.07% leaves out the 2.37%, and 9.17% is an old pre-2015 combined rate.

2. What is the rate on direct-to-home satellite service?
Show the answer

Answer: 11.44% with no local tax. Satellite is taxed at 9.07% + 2.37% = 11.44%, and local tax does not apply.

3. A family's home landline is billed $40. Which parts of CST apply?
Show the answer

Answer: Only the 2.37% gross receipts tax and the local tax. The residential exemption removes only the 4.92% state tax and the 0.15% gross receipts tax.

4. Which service can get the residential partial exemption?
Show the answer

Answer: A home VoIP line. Mobile, video and satellite service never get the exemption. A home VoIP line can.
