Module 5: Which services are taxable

Level: Intermediate · About 30 minutes · Module 5 of 17

Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

  • Apply the legal definition of communications services to a charge.
  • List the services the Department names as taxable.
  • List the services and charges that are outside CST.
  • Use a three-question flowchart to classify any charge.

The definition

“Communications services” means the transmission, conveyance or routing of voice, data, audio, video or any other information or signals, including video services, to a point or between points, by any medium, whatever the protocol. It expressly includes VoIP, even when computer processing is used. [s. 202.11, F.S.] [CST page]

The definition then excludes eight things, which are not communications services: [s. 202.11, F.S.] [DR-700016N]

  • information services (such as electronic publishing, web hosting and end-user 900-number services)
  • installation or maintenance of wiring or equipment on the customer's premises
  • the sale or rental of tangible personal property (the phone, the cable box)
  • advertising, including directory advertising
  • bad check charges
  • late payment charges
  • billing and collection services
  • Internet access, email, electronic bulletin board and similar online computer services

Some of these, such as the sale or rental of equipment, may be subject to sales tax instead. [DR-700016N]

Services and charges outside CST

Besides the exclusions in the definition, the sales price of communications services does not include: [s. 202.11, F.S.] [DR-700016N]

  • the sale or recharge of a prepaid calling arrangement (taxed under the sales tax law instead; Module 11)
  • calls paid for by inserting coins into a public coin-operated phone
  • air-to-ground service provided on board an aircraft
  • a dealer's internal use of communications services in its communications business

Cases from the Department's questions and answers

  • Hotel pay-per-view movies are a taxable communications service. [DOR FAQ 1276]
  • Fax service is taxable, unless it is provided in the course of professional or advertising services. [DOR FAQ 1316] [TIP 08A19-01]
  • End-user 900-number services and other information services are not subject to CST. [DOR FAQ 1271] [DOR FAQ 1284]
  • A mobile home park that buys satellite service and resells it to residents as cable service is a dealer: it must register and collect tax, and can buy the service for resale. [DOR FAQ 1270]
  • A condominium or timeshare association, or an RV or mobile home park, that includes cable service in its fees without separately stating a charge for it does not collect CST from residents. [DOR FAQ 1294] [DOR FAQ 1369]

Key takeaways

  • CST covers transmission of signals by any medium, including VoIP, video, satellite and mobile.
  • Information services, Internet access, equipment, advertising, billing and late or returned-check charges are not communications services.
  • Prepaid calling, coin phones, air-to-ground service and a dealer's internal use are left out of the sales price.
  • When in doubt, ask: does it transmit signals, is it excluded, is it exempt?

Self-check

Choose an answer for each question, then select Check answer. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

1. Which is excluded from the definition of communications services?
Show the answer

Answer: Internet access. Internet access, email and similar online services are excluded.

2. A hotel charges a guest for a pay-per-view movie. Is it subject to CST?
Show the answer

Answer: Yes. The Department's FAQ says hotel pay-per-view movies are subject to CST.

3. A law firm faxes documents for a client as part of its legal services. Is CST due on the fax?
Show the answer

Answer: No, fax in the course of professional services is not taxable. Fax is taxable except when provided in the course of professional or advertising services.

4. A condominium association includes cable in its monthly fee without stating a separate charge. Does it collect CST from owners?
Show the answer

Answer: No. Per the Department's FAQ, it does not collect CST when the cable charge is not separately stated.
