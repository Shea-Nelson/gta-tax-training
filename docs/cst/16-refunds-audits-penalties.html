Module 16: Refunds, bad debts, audits and penalties

Level: Master · About 30 minutes · Module 16 of 17

Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

  • Walk a customer's refund request through the dealer and the Department.
  • Know a dealer's deadline to take credit for tax it overpaid.
  • Compute late-filing penalties and find the current interest rate.
  • Describe the stages and deadlines of a CST audit.

Customer refunds: the procedure in s. 202.23

A customer who believes CST was collected in error, for example on a nontaxable charge, at too high a rate, on an exempt sale, or for the wrong local jurisdiction, must send the dealer a signed written request within 3 years after the tax was collected. [s. 202.23, F.S.]

  1. Within 30 days of a complete request, the dealer decides whether any of the tax was collected because of an error. It refunds or credits that amount within 45 days.
  2. For any amount it does not refund, it sends a copy of the request to the Department within those 30 days.
  3. The Department decides within 90 days, and the dealer refunds or credits any amount the Department finds not due within 45 days after the Department's notice.

This procedure is the customer's sole and exclusive remedy. A customer can sue only after using it and showing that the dealer or government did not follow it, and the suit must be filed within 180 days after the dealer's written response, or within 1 year after the request if the dealer never gave a timely response. [s. 202.23, F.S.]

Dealer credits and refunds

A dealer that overpaid tax can take a credit or ask for a refund within 3 years after it remitted the tax, or within 60 days after it refunded the tax to a customer, whichever is later. Local tax credits are reported for the jurisdiction where the tax was originally reported. [s. 202.23, F.S.] Bad debt credits follow the 12-month rule in Module 14. [s. 202.29, F.S.] [TIP 04A19-01]

Penalties

A late return or late payment brings a penalty of 10% of the unpaid tax for each 30 days or fraction of a 30-day period, up to 50%. [CST page] [s. 202.28, F.S.] [DR-700016N]

Other CST penalties include $5,000 per return for not reporting local tax on Schedule I, 10% (up to $10,000 per return) for wrong-jurisdiction assignments without a protected method (Module 8), and criminal penalties for willful evasion. Knowingly failing, with intent to evade tax, to file six consecutive returns is a third-degree felony. [s. 202.28, F.S.]

Interest

Interest is due on late payments from the date the tax was due until it is paid, at a floating rate updated each January 1 and July 1 under s. 213.235, F.S. [DR-700016N] [CST page] The annual rate is 11%, a daily rate factor of 0.000301370, for July 1 through December 31, 2026 [TIP 26ADM-02] and again for January 1 through June 30, 2027. [TIP 26ADM-06] Both TIPs list communications services tax among the taxes the rate applies to. Check the tax and interest rates page for later periods. [Tax and interest rates page]

Lighthouse Mobile pays 45 days late

Fictional business; interest shown only as the method. Lighthouse Mobile files and pays its August 2026 return, with $40,000.00 on Line 5, 45 days after the September 21, 2026 deadline (September 20 was a Sunday). [DR-700016N] [s. 202.28, F.S.] [TIP 26ADM-02]

  1. Collection allowance: none, because the return was late.
  2. Penalty: 45 days is in the 31-to-60 band, so 20% × $40,000.00 = $8,000.00 on Line 8.
  3. Interest on Line 9: unpaid tax × 0.000301370 × days late, using the rate for each period the tax was unpaid.

Audits

CST audits follow the same process as sales and use tax audits. [GT-800022] [GT-800023] The sales and use tax guide walks through the same stages with a worked example in SUT Module 16.

  1. Notice. The Department sends a Notice of Intent to Audit Books and Records (DR-840). Audit work cannot begin for 60 days, unless the taxpayer waives the waiting period.
  2. Entrance interview. The auditor explains the audit plan and asks about the business and its records. A representative needs a power of attorney (DR-835).
  3. The audit. The usual lookback is three years, longer if returns were not filed or were substantially incorrect. Records may be paper or electronic; records kept electronically must be provided electronically. A taxpayer can ask for a Technical Assistance Advisement during the audit.
  4. Findings. The Department generally must issue its assessment within 305 days after the DR-840. The taxpayer receives a Notice of Intent to Make Audit Changes (DR-1215) and should respond within 30 days.
  5. Assessment. About 30 days after the DR-1215, a Notice of Proposed Assessment follows. The taxpayer has 60 days to file an informal protest or 120 days for a formal one.

CST audits look closely at how service addresses are assigned and which rates are applied, and the law allows the Department to use sampling in audits. [s. 202.37, F.S.] [s. 202.34, F.S.] A city or county may also audit a dealer that does business only within that county, under conditions in the law. [s. 202.37, F.S.]

Key takeaways

  • Customer refunds: written request to the dealer within 3 years; dealer 30 and 45 days; Department 90 days.
  • Dealer credits: 3 years from remittance or 60 days after refunding a customer.
  • Late penalty: 10% per 30 days, up to 50%; interest floats each January and July.
  • Audits: DR-840, 60-day wait, three-year lookback, DR-1215, then the Notice of Proposed Assessment.

Self-check

Choose an answer for each question, then select Check answer. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

1. How long does a customer have to request a refund of CST from the dealer?
Show the answer

Answer: 3 years after collection. The written request must be submitted within 3 years after the tax was collected.

2. A return is filed and paid 45 days late. What is the late penalty rate?
Show the answer

Answer: 20%. 10% for each 30 days or fraction: 45 days is two periods, so 20%.

3. What starts the 60-day pre-audit period?
Show the answer

Answer: A Notice of Intent to Audit Books and Records (DR-840). The DR-840 starts the 60-day period, which the taxpayer may waive.

4. What is the usual audit lookback period?
Show the answer

Answer: Three years. Typically three years, longer for non-filers or substantially incorrect returns.
