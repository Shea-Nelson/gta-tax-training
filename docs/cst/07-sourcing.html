Module 7: Where a service is taxed: the service address

Level: Intermediate · About 25 minutes · Module 7 of 17

Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

  • Find the service address for fixed, card-billed, video, satellite and mobile service.
  • Explain a mobile provider's duty to keep each customer's place of primary use.
  • Compute the Florida taxable amount of a private line circuit that crosses the state line.

Why the service address matters

Florida CST applies to a service that originates and terminates in Florida, or that originates or terminates in Florida and is charged to a service address in Florida. [s. 202.12, F.S.] The local tax applies to services charged to a service address in the city or county that levies it. [s. 202.19, F.S.] So the service address decides both whether Florida tax applies and which local rate is charged.

The service address rules

The law defines the service address differently for each kind of service: [s. 202.11, F.S.]

  • General rule: the location of the communications equipment from which the service originates or at which the customer receives it.
  • Calls paid by bank, travel, debit or credit card, and calling-card or third-number calls: the address of the central office, identified by the area code and the first three digits of the seven-digit originating number.
  • If neither applies because the equipment's location is not known: the location of the customer's primary use, meaning the customer's residential or business street address.
  • Video and direct-to-home satellite service: the location in Florida where the customer receives the service.
  • Mobile service: the customer's place of primary use.

Mobile service: the place of primary use

The place of primary use is the street address that represents where the customer mostly uses the mobile service. It must be the customer's residential street address or primary business street address, and it must be within the licensed service area of the customer's home service provider. [s. 202.11, F.S.] This follows the federal Mobile Telecommunications Sourcing Act: Florida taxes mobile service the Act treats as provided by a home service provider to a customer whose service address is in Florida. [s. 202.12, F.S.]

The home service provider must obtain and keep each customer's place of primary use. If it relies in good faith on the street address the customer gives, it may use that address and is held harmless from additional tax based on a different address. If the Department, after notice and a final order, decides an address or jurisdiction assignment is wrong, the provider must begin using the correct one within 120 days. [s. 202.155, F.S.]

Mobile bundles follow the same books-and-records idea as Module 6: a nontaxable mobile service billed together with taxable service is treated as taxable unless the home service provider can reasonably identify its price from its regular books and records. [s. 202.155, F.S.]

Private line service crossing the state line

Private communications service gives the customer exclusive or priority use of a channel between channel termination points. [s. 202.11, F.S.] It is taxed at the Florida rates on: [s. 202.12, F.S.]

  1. any charge for a channel termination point in Florida;
  2. any charge for a channel between two termination points in Florida; and
  3. where termination points are both in and outside Florida:
    • 50% of the charge for any separately billed segment between two such points; or
    • if a segment is not separately billed, the total circuit charge times the number of termination points in Florida divided by the total number of termination points.

The local tax on private communications service has its own rule in s. 202.19, F.S., based on where the termination points are. [s. 202.19, F.S.]

Where is each charge sourced?

Fictional customers and businesses. [s. 202.11, F.S.] [s. 202.12, F.S.] [s. 202.155, F.S.]

  • Maria lives in Gainesville and her mobile plan is billed to that home address. On a week's trip to Miami she makes most of her calls there. Her place of primary use is still her Gainesville residential street address, so Gainesville's local rate applies.
  • A dental office has two desk phones at its office in Ocala. The equipment's location is the service address.
  • A satellite TV customer with a Georgia billing address receives service at a lake house in Florida. Florida is where it is received, so the 11.44% applies.
  • Seaside Data sells a private line with three termination points, two in Florida and one in Alabama, for $900.00 a month, not billed by segment. Florida taxable amount: $900.00 × 2/3 = $600.00.

Key takeaways

  • Fixed service: where the equipment is. Card calls: the central office. Video and satellite: where received.
  • Mobile: the customer's residential or primary business street address in the provider's licensed area.
  • A mobile provider may rely in good faith on the customer's address, and must correct it within 120 days of a final order.
  • Interstate private lines: 50% of a separately billed segment, or a termination-point fraction.

Self-check

Choose an answer for each question, then select Check answer. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

1. What is the service address for mobile service?
Show the answer

Answer: The customer's place of primary use. Mobile service is sourced to the place of primary use: the residential or primary business street address in the licensed service area.

2. Satellite TV is received at a Florida home but billed to an Ohio address. Where is it sourced?
Show the answer

Answer: The Florida location where it is received. Video and satellite service are sourced to where they are received in Florida.

3. A calling-card call's service address is:
Show the answer

Answer: The central office for the area code and first three digits of the originating number. Under s. 202.11(14)(a)2., card and third-number calls are sourced to the central office identified by the area code and first three digits of the originating number, not to the caller's home or the card issuer.

4. A private line circuit has 3 termination points in Florida and 1 out of state, $1,000 a month, not billed by segment. What is the Florida taxable amount?
Show the answer

Answer: $750. When a private line is not billed by segment, Florida taxes the share of termination points in Florida: 3 of 4 points, so $1,000 × 3/4 = $750.
