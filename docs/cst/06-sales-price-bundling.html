Module 6: Sales price, bundles and charges on the bill

Level: Intermediate · About 25 minutes · Module 6 of 17

Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

  • Define the taxable sales price of a communications service.
  • Name the charges that are always taxable, even when listed separately.
  • Name the charges left out of the sales price.
  • Apply the rule for a bundle that mixes taxable and nontaxable services.

Sales price: the whole charge

Tax is computed on the sales price: the total amount charged for the right to use communications services in Florida, including any property or other service sold with it, unless that other charge is separately itemized on the bill or allocated under the books-and-records rule below. [s. 202.11, F.S.] [DR-700016N]

A dealer cannot reduce the sales price by its own expenses that it chooses to list on the bill, such as sales tax it paid on its purchases, property taxes, income taxes or universal service fund fees. [s. 202.11, F.S.]

Charges that are always taxable

These charges are part of the sales price whether or not they are stated separately: [s. 202.11, F.S.] [DR-700016N]

  1. connecting, moving, changing or ending service
  2. detailed billing
  3. directory listings sold with a communications service
  4. central office and custom calling features
  5. voice mail and other messaging services
  6. directory assistance
  7. sending or receiving a fax, except as part of professional or advertising services

Charges left out of the sales price

The sales price does not include: [s. 202.11, F.S.]

  • separately stated taxes on the service, including CST itself and gross receipts tax
  • separately stated government fees, such as regulatory fees and 911 emergency telephone surcharges
  • coin-operated public telephone calls
  • the sale or recharge of a prepaid calling arrangement
  • air-to-ground service
  • a dealer's internal use of communications services
  • separately stated charges for property or other services that are not part of the communications service
  • nontaxable goods or services, such as Internet access, that are not separately itemized but can be reasonably identified from the dealer's books and records (see below)

Bundles: taxable and nontaxable services for one price

When a dealer sells taxable communications services together with nontaxable services for a single price, the general rule is that the whole charge is taxable. [DR-700016N] [s. 202.11, F.S.] The exception: the nontaxable portion is not taxed if the dealer can reasonably identify it from books and records kept in the regular course of business. [s. 202.11, F.S.] [DR-700016N] The nontaxable portion may still be subject to sales tax under Chapter 212. [DR-700016N]

This exception began on July 1, 2005, when Florida changed its law to comply with federal requirements for Internet access. Books and records include general ledgers, price lists, cost records, customer billings, billing system reports, tariffs and regulatory filings, and they may cover the dealer's whole service area, including areas outside Florida. [TIP 05A19-03] [s. 202.11, F.S.]

The books-and-records rule never removes the always-taxable charges listed above. A bundle price cannot be used to move a connection fee or voice mail out of the tax. [s. 202.11, F.S.]

Pelican Fiber's $120 bundle

Fictional business. Pelican Fiber sells a $120.00 “triple play” of TV channels, a home phone line and Internet access for one price, with no breakdown on the bill. [s. 202.11, F.S.] [TIP 05A19-03] [DR-700016N]

  • If Pelican cannot identify the Internet portion from its records: CST applies to the full $120.00.
  • If its regular price list and billing system show the bundle as $70.00 video, $20.00 phone and $30.00 Internet: it may exclude the $30.00. It taxes $70.00 of video at 5.07% + 2.37% + local, and the $20.00 residential phone line at 2.37% + local.
  • If the bundle also included a $5.00 voice mail feature, that $5.00 stays taxable no matter how it is shown.

Key takeaways

  • Tax the total charge, and do not deduct the dealer's own costs.
  • Connection, detailed billing, listings, features, voice mail, directory assistance and fax are always taxable.
  • Separately stated government taxes and fees are not part of the sales price.
  • In a bundle, a nontaxable part is excluded only if the dealer's regular books and records identify it.

Self-check

Choose an answer for each question, then select Check answer. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

1. Which charge is taxable even if separately stated on the bill?
Show the answer

Answer: Voice mail. Voice mail is one of the charges always included in the sales price.

2. A dealer lists its universal service fund fee as a separate line. May it exclude that amount from the sales price?
Show the answer

Answer: No. Dealer expenses such as USF fees do not reduce the sales price.

3. A bundle of TV and Internet access is sold for one price with no breakdown. When can the Internet portion be left out of the tax?
Show the answer

Answer: When the dealer can reasonably identify it from books and records kept in the regular course of business. Under s. 202.11(13)(b)8., the nontaxable part of a bundle (here, Internet access) can be excluded only if the dealer can reasonably identify it from books and records kept in the regular course of business. Otherwise the whole bundled price is taxable.

4. Which is not part of the CST sales price?
Show the answer

Answer: The sale or recharge of a prepaid calling arrangement. Prepaid calling arrangement sales are excluded from the CST sales price.
