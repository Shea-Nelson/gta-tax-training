Module 3: Local CST and computing the tax on a bill

Level: Beginner · About 30 minutes · Module 3 of 17

Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

  • Explain who levies local CST and the limits on its rate.
  • Build a jurisdiction's total local rate from its adopted rate and its county surtax conversion rate.
  • Find a current local rate in the Department's rate table.
  • Compute and round the tax on a bill using the three-decimal rounding rule.

Who levies local CST

Each local taxing jurisdiction (a municipality, a charter county, or the unincorporated area of a county) has its own local CST rate. [GT-800011] [s. 202.19, F.S.] The law caps the rate a jurisdiction may adopt: [s. 202.19, F.S.]

  • Municipalities and charter counties: up to 5.1%, or up to 4.98% if they have chosen to levy permit fees.
  • Noncharter counties: up to 1.6%.
  • These caps do not include add-ons of up to 0.12% (municipalities and charter counties) or 0.24% (noncharter counties) for governments that do not levy permit fees, and they do not limit the conversion or emergency rates the law allows.

The local tax takes the place of the fees a city or county would otherwise charge dealers for using public rights-of-way. [s. 202.19, F.S.] The rate in effect on January 1, 2023 may not be increased before January 1, 2031 (Module 15 explains this freeze). [s. 202.19, F.S.] [TIP 25A19-01]

Adopted rate + surtax conversion rate = total local rate

A jurisdiction's total local rate has two parts: [CST rate workbook] [s. 202.19, F.S.] [s. 202.21, F.S.]

  1. the adopted rate, set by the city or county; and
  2. a county surtax conversion rate, which reflects the county's discretionary sales surtax, converted into an equivalent CST rate under s. 202.20(3), F.S.

The Department always publishes the total of the two, and that total is the rate a dealer charges. [s. 202.21, F.S.] Any discretionary sales surtax increase on or after January 1, 2023 may not be added to the local CST before January 1, 2031. [s. 202.19, F.S.]

Reading the rate table

The Department publishes a current “Communications Services Tax Rate Table” and a workbook of historical, current and upcoming local rates. [GT-800011] [CST rate table] [CST rate workbook] Each row of the workbook gives the county, the jurisdiction, the adopted rate, the county surtax conversion rate, the total local rate, effective and expiration dates (“NONE” for a rate still in effect), and whether the jurisdiction elected to levy permit fees. Current rates are in bold and upcoming rates are in red bold. [CST rate workbook] In the January 2026 workbook, current total local rates ran from 0.30% to 7.70%.

Rounding: computing the tax on a bill

Since 2011 CST uses a standard rounding method: carry the computation to the third decimal place, and round up to the next whole cent when the third decimal is 5 or greater. For example, $5.045 rounds to $5.05 and $3.213 rounds to $3.21. [TIP 11A19-01] [s. 202.16, F.S.]

The local tax is always rounded separately from the Florida CST. The Florida CST (state and gross receipts) can be rounded in any one of three ways: [TIP 11A19-01] [s. 202.16, F.S.]

  1. on the combined state and gross receipts rate;
  2. on the state rate, separately from the combined gross receipts rate; or
  3. on the combined state rate and 0.15% gross receipts rate (5.07%), separately from the 2.37% gross receipts rate.

A dealer may round on the invoice total or on each taxable item, but if it rounds item by item, the result must be at least what rounding the invoice total would give. Dealers may set a minimum tax of at least $0.01 on each item. [TIP 11A19-01]

Worked example: Sunrise Voice's $49.99 business line

Fictional business. Sunrise Voice bills a $49.99 business line to a customer in Stuart, where the total local rate is 5.22% from January 1, 2026. It rounds using option 3. [CST rate workbook] [TIP 11A19-01]

  1. 5.07% state: $49.99 × 0.0507 = $2.534 (carried to three decimals). The third decimal, 4, is less than 5, so $2.53.
  2. 2.37% gross receipts: $49.99 × 0.0237 = $1.184, so $1.18.
  3. Local: $49.99 × 0.0522 = $2.609. The third decimal is 9, so round up: $2.61.
  4. The bill shows Florida CST $3.71 ($2.53 + $1.18) and local CST $2.61, a total of $6.32.

Key takeaways

  • Every city, charter county and unincorporated area has its own local rate, capped by law.
  • Total local rate = adopted rate + county surtax conversion rate.
  • Round to the cent only when the third decimal is 5 or more, and round local tax separately.
  • Look up the rate in effect for the bill date in the Department's rate table.

Self-check

Choose an answer for each question, then select Check answer. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

1. A jurisdiction's total local rate is made of:
Show the answer

Answer: Its adopted rate plus the county surtax conversion rate. The Department publishes the adopted rate plus the county surtax conversion rate as one total.

2. Tax computes to $2.534. What is charged?
Show the answer

Answer: $2.53. Carry to three decimals; round up only if the third decimal is 5 or more. 4 is less than 5.

3. Which must always be rounded separately?
Show the answer

Answer: The local tax, apart from the Florida CST. Local tax is always rounded separately; the Florida CST has three permitted rounding options.

4. Until when may a local CST rate in effect on January 1, 2023 not be increased?
Show the answer

Answer: January 1, 2031. Section 202.19(2)(d) bars increases before January 1, 2031.
